# Migration and Settlement

This section examines how individual bearers of the surname moved between the Oryol, Kursk, Livny, Taurida and Crimean regions.

## What Has Already Been Proved

- **Oryol district → Shchigry in Livny district:** in his personal skazka (written statement) of 1700, Akim Ivanovich **Anpilogov** explicitly named his former pomestye (service estate) in Bozdyrevo, its forcible seizure, and the 30 chetverti held by his grandson Tit in Shchigry. The original has been checked at ll. 1181–1182 of RGADA, f. 210, op. 12, d. 1888.

## What Is Probable but Needs a Direct Document

- **Kursk → Oboyan:** Sergey Grigoryevich, bearing the rare surname, appears in the Kursk desyatnya (muster roll) of 1636 and the Oboyan desyatnya of 1651. It still has to be proved that this is one and the same man, and his transfer or resettlement has to be documented.

## What Is So Far Only a Line of Search

- **Kursk–Oryol region → mainland Taurida:** in the 19th century state peasants are known to have migrated from the central black-earth governorates, and bearers of the surname appear in Melitopol district. No specific migrant family has been identified.
- **Mainland Taurida → Crimea:** in Simferopol, the family of Sergey Ampilogov from Rylsk district is confirmed by a parish register entry of 1868 (`FS-3Q9M-CS9N-9FBF`). Its connection with Troitskoye or Tymoshivka, and any route through mainland Taurida, have not been established.

## The Rule

Each route gets a separate dossier. It must contain:

1. the point of departure;
2. the point of arrival;
3. the person or family;
4. the latest document at the point of departure;
5. the earliest document at the point of arrival;
6. a document directly linking the two points;
7. alternative explanations.
