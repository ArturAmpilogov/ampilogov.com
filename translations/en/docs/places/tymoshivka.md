# Tymoshivka

## Names

The sources and modern databases use the following forms:

- Тимошевка (Timoshevka);
- Тимошовка (Timoshovka);
- Тимошівка (Tymoshivka);
- Tymoshivka.

Historically the village belonged to Melitopol district (uyezd) of Taurida governorate. In this book the administrative form is always tied to the date of the document.

Present-day location: the village of Tymoshivka in the Mykhailivka settlement hromada (community), Vasylivka raion, Zaporizhzhia oblast, Ukraine. This distinguishes it from other places of the same name. [Official reference directory of the administrative-territorial structure of Ukraine](https://decentralization.ua/newgromada/3931?page=2).

## Role in the research on resettlement

Tymoshivka is more than a cluster of the surname in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. A primary register sheet records here the death in 1836 of the 85-year-old settler (poselyanin) Ilya Ioannovich Anpilogov, and the registers of 1816–1844 name the adult Aleksey, Pavel, Ioann and Timofey Ilyich. This carries the documented depth of the local group back to a man born about 1751, and makes the search for the family in the resettlement registers of 1809–1810 the main line of inquiry.

The founding history distinguishes a single first settler from a mass wave. In 1809 the odnodvorets peasant Timofey Timoshenko, who came from the military settlements of the Dnieper Line, founded a khutor in the Matogay tract; in 1810 state peasants from Oboyan, Belgorod, Korocha, Sudzha and Tim districts of Kursk governorate settled around it. The Kursk–Belgorod area therefore remains the best general hypothesis for Ilya's family, with the military slobodas (settlements) on the Konka as an additional but weaker control. The exact district has not yet been established. One particularly testable trace has been found in the sloboda of Trekhizbenskaya in Bakhmut province. A secondary publication based on the materials of the first revision (census) of 1719–1724 names Yevdokim Trofimov Anpilogov there as the eldest of his family. The surname does not appear in the open index to the 1744 revision, while the by-surname index to the third revision of 1762 again shows a single household of Anpilov odnodvortsy. This household has been found in an open primary scan from ГАВО (State Archive of Voronezh Oblast), f. I-18, op. 1, d. 103, l. 204, No. 1169: the retired dragoon Onisim Anpilov, 70, his wife Praskovya Fyodorovna, 50, and their sons Ivan, 14, and Andrey, 12. There is no Ilya Ivanov syn (Ilya, son of Ivan) of about 11 in this only household, and the name of its head does not match the patronymic of Ilya Ioannovich of Tymoshivka. The direct version Trekhizbenskaya → Tymoshivka is therefore considerably weakened. For an early control there remains ГАВО, f. I-18, op. 1, d. 29, ll. 91–477, 683, for 1744–1746. A late cross-section is even more important: ГАВО, f. I-18, op. 1, d. 175 contains registers of the Cossacks of Trekhizbenskaya for 1797–1801 on ll. 79–165 — only eight to twelve years before Tymoshivka was founded. It has not yet been established whether the case covers all the odnodvortsy, so the absence of the surname in it would not by itself prove resettlement.

The northern trace of Onisim himself, which seemed strong, has also been checked. The census of 1718 does indeed name in the village of Gornyaya in Stary Oskol district the pikeman (kopeyshchik) Anisim Iyevlev syn Anpilov. But the third revision of 1762 continues this very household in the village of Arkhangelskoye: Anisim was listed as 65 in the previous revision and died in 1750, leaving a son, Rodion. He therefore could not have been the 70-year-old retired dragoon Onisim living in Trekhizbenskaya in 1762. The rare name and surname here belong to two different people; the route Stary Oskol → Trekhizbenskaya is withdrawn. Neither of the two households contains the Ilya Ivanov syn born about 1751 who is being sought.

At the same time another Stary Oskol trace has become more precise, though its archival reference needs checking. A published fragment of the original of the sixth revision shows in 1811 the 64-year-old Ivan Ivanov syn Anpilov in the «вновь начинающей деревне Анпиловой» ("newly beginning village of Anpilova"); the researcher places it near the village of Bor. He was born about 1747 — only four years before Ilya Ioannovich of Tymoshivka — and has the same patronymic. This does not prove that they were brothers, but it creates a precise test. The revision address has already been narrowed: Bor begins on frame 61 and Sorokino on frame 86 of ГАКО (State Archive of Kursk Oblast) case 184-2-103 for 1762; Bor in 1782 begins on electronic frame 392 of case 184-2-149. For the fifth revision both neighbouring Stary Oskol volumes, 184-2-204 and 184-2-205, need to be checked: case 205 is expressly titled as the tales (skazki) of the odnodvortsy, but neighbouring Neznamovo has already been located in case 204. A published comparison specifies that there is as yet no separate Anpilova in the fifth revision: the households in question should be sought within the Bor section. There one must check for an Ilya Ivanov syn of about 44; in the sixth revision, for a note of his departure to Taurida governorate. The correct Stary Oskol set for 1811 has been found: ГАКО 184-2-353, revision tales of the odnodvortsy and state settlers of the district, 1,064 leaves. The neighbouring references leave a very narrow probable interval for Bor and the newly formed Anpilova: after Brodok on ll. 57–62 and before Baranovo on ll. 71–76, that is, ll. 63–70. This is a reconstruction of the address, not a heading already read; the exact leaf of the published frame has not yet been established. The fragment was earlier wrongly attributed to ГАКО case 184-2-346. Its geographical index puts the hamlet (seltso) of Anpilogovo on page 13 in the block of the Kursk suburbs; the small Stary Oskol block begins at about page 321 and does not contain Bor. This is a separate centre, not a confirmation of the Bor location of the frame.
[PUB-GAKO-184-2-346-ANPILOVA-IVAN-ANPILOV-1811]

In the Kursk area another early pocket of the surname has now been confirmed from primary sources. ГАКО 184-2-91, electronic scans 740–742 and 756–757, shows three households in the village of Sakolova in 1762: those of Ivan Dementyevich and Gavriil Tarasovich Anpilogov and Ivan Larionovich Onpilogov. Sakolova and Anpilogovo are later listed as different names; they must not be merged. Moreover, the printed parish distances do not agree with the two known cartographic locations of Anpilogova, so immediate proximity to the particular 16-household village has not yet been proved. The male lines have now been traced further. Ivan Dementyevich has the sons Yegor (9 → 26), Mikhail (3 → 20), Foma (15) and Pyotr (10); Ivan Larionovich has Antip (24 → 41), a grandson Mikhail with the note «в бегах безвестно» ("fled, whereabouts unknown") and Fyodor (5 → 22). There is no suitable Ilya Ivanov syn among the confidently read men; it is particularly significant that the ten-year-old boy is named Pyotr. In Nikolskoye the household of Ivan Yepifanovich was also checked: he died in 1758, and his son Fyodor is shown as twenty in 1762; there is no clear Ilya in the readable male part. These two results narrow the search for a specific father, but at the same time show that the surname must be sought as a network of neighbouring households, not only by the place name Anpilogova.

To get round the gap between revisions, a precise church source has been found. The official inventory of ГАКО confirms case 217-1-1901 — the parish register (metricheskaya kniga) of the Pokrovskaya (Intercession) church of the village of Balychevo for 1782–1820, 321 leaves. This is a bridge in time where marriages, deaths, younger children and godparents of the Sakolova households can be sought not only by surname but by the patronymics Dementyev, Tarasov and Larionov and through the Kalugin network. The composition of the parish in 1908 cannot be carried back to 1782: the internal headings of the book itself must be read first. The ГАКО electronic archive system (ЕАИС) requires authorisation, and the archive designates remote viewing of scans from fonds 184 and 217 as a paid service; the case has therefore been located but is not counted as viewed.
[PUB-GAKO-217-1-1901-BALYCHEVO-METRICS-1782-1820]

Between the revision cross-sections, an independent primary confirmation of the family settlement itself has been found. The economic notes to the General Land Survey of Stary Oskol district, РГИА (Russian State Historical Archive), f. 1350, op. 312, d. 84, l. 95–95 v., in the 1780s expressly name the «деревню с хутором Анпиловы» ("village with a khutor, Anpilovy") owned by the Anpilov odnodvortsy. The village is recorded with 9 households and 24 revision souls — 12 male and 12 female; the khutor adds a tenth household, but no separate number of souls is entered in its line. This reliably fills the gap between the Bor families of 1762 and the "newly beginning" Anpilova of 1811, but it does not name Ilya and does not prove resettlement to Tymoshivka.
[PUB-RGIA-1350-312-84-ANPILOVA-ECONOMIC-NOTES-1782]

A local-history chronicle attributes the founding of Anpilovka to Anofry Karpovich Anpilov in 1723 and correctly reproduces the 1782 total — 10 households and 24 inhabitants. But its early date remains disputed: the complete list of Bor household heads in the census of 1723 contains no Anpilovs, and the primary economic notes do not name a founder. What is therefore regarded as proved is that the settlement existed by the 1780s, but not that it was founded in 1723.

In the context of the same publication, two primary fragments of the third revision were found that had not previously been entered in the card. Both are headed «села Бору» ("of the village of Bor") and show separate lines for the odnodvortsy Ivan Demidov syn and Ivan Ivanov syn Anpilov. This turns the Stary Oskol version from a general coincidence of surname into a specific family test: the continuation of both lines should show whether either household included an Ilya Ivanov syn of about eleven. However, only cropped line headings have been published, without wives, children or ages; the two Ivans can neither be taken as father and son nor can either of them be identified with the later Ivan Ivanovich born in 1747.

The starting node of this branch has now been confirmed by a primary fragment. On leaf 414 of the census book of 1719, Denis and his brother Ivan Anpilov are named as joint owners of a mill on the Oskol near the village of Sorokino. This agrees with an independent comparison of the revisions, according to which Anpilovs moved from Sorokino to Bor between the first and second revisions. A primary fragment of 1762 now confirms Ivan Demidov syn Anpilov in Bor; a report by his descendant dates his birth to about 1717. The same descendant separately names the co-owners of the Sorokino mill as Ivan Ivanovich (1661–1721) and Denis Ivanovich (1666–1720), and for Ivan Ivanovich of the sixth revision gives the dates 1747–1825. This is a useful private genealogy, but not a reading of the published fragments. Until the complete households of 1762 are shown, Ivan Demidovich cannot be declared the father either of the later Ivan Ivanovich or of Ilya Ivanovich of Tymoshivka, born about 1751; the decisive test is to check the continuations of both lines for Ilya.
[PUB-RGADA-350-1-384-SOROKINO-ANPILOV-MILL-1719]

A broader source-critical control shows that this centre cannot be reduced to Sorokino alone. According to an expert summary of the early documents and the first revision, at the beginning of the eighteenth century there were at least nine Anpilov households: five in Gornyaya, two in Sorokino, one in Yamskaya sloboda and one more near it. At the same time a popular legend is corrected: the line in the estimate book (smetnaya kniga) of 1668 names only Gerasim Anpilov with his son Stenka and does not place them on a khutor near Podgornaya Polyana. The origin of the Bor branch must therefore be checked not by a single place name but through the patronymic Demid → Ivan Demidovich in all four groups. Gornyaya has already become a negative control on the basis of the complete families of 1762; the main unexplored reserve is the two Sorokino and two Yamskaya sloboda households of the first revision. [PUB-VGD-STARY-OSKOL-ANPILOV-DISPERSION-1629-1723]

An earlier patronymic bridge has also appeared. A deed book (krepostnaya kniga) of 1703 twice names a witness — «староосколец сын боярской Денис Иванов сын Анпилов» ("a man of Stary Oskol, syn boyarsky, Denis Ivanov syn Anpilov"). He is a strong candidate for the miller Denis of 1719: the name, the rare surname, the area and the generation all match. If this is one man, then Denis of Sorokino was an Ivanovich, and Ivan, named as his brother, belonged to the same group of "Ivanoviches" that the first revision places in Sorokino. But the 1719 fragment does not show Denis's patronymic, so this identification remains a testable hypothesis, not a finished genealogy.
[PUB-VGD-RGADA-615-1-10661-L51-DENIS-ANPILOV-1703]

Another candidate, independent of Stary Oskol, has been found in the village of Krasnoye in the Bruslanovsky stan (subdistrict) of Yelets district. A published tale (skazka) of 1722 names Miron Ilyin syn Anpilov, his sons Ivan and Danila and his grandson Ivan Ivanov syn. Ivan Anpilov recurs in the oath list of 1742; in 1755 he is expressly called Ivan Ivanov syn, and in the third revision of 1762 he stands as a separate household head. The second household head at that time was the retired corporal Danila Mironovich Anpilov. In the confession list (ispovednaya vedomost) of 1792 the 51-year-old Vasily Ivanovich Anpilov is named as head of the next generation.

This is so far the most precise test of the presumed father of Ilya Ivanovich of Tymoshivka: Ivan Ivanovich lived in the right generation, and Vasily, born about 1741, could have been the elder brother of Ilya, born about 1751. But the composition of Ivan's household is omitted from the published extract. The decisive address is РГАДА (Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts), f. 350, op. 2, d. 955, l. 656–656 v.: there one must look for a son Ilya of about 10–12. Ilya's absence among the family heads of 1792 merely allows for an early departure and does not by itself prove resettlement. The control of the remaining branches has already been located: the fifth revision of Krasnoye of 1795 occupies ll. 28–31 in ГАТО (State Archive of Tambov Oblast), f. 12, op. 1, d. 208, and the sixth revision of 1811 ll. 133–148 in d. 411. Both cases have already been digitised by the ГАТО electronic reading room. This makes it possible to check not only whether Ilya is present, but also the previous ages, household numbers and direct formulas of departure between the two censuses. Viewing the images requires registration and paid access time; a primary transcription of these leaves has not yet been made. The later lists of household heads have added an important counter-argument. In the eighth revision of Krasnoye of 1834, Lukyan Antonov and Ivan Ilyin Anpilov are recorded side by side; in the tenth revision of 1858, Ion Lukyanov and again Ivan Ilyin Anpilov. So a local Ilya branch of its own continued in Krasnoye. This does not exclude the resettlement of another Ilya Ivanovich, but it requires first establishing the father and age of the local Ilya rather than identifying him with the Tymoshivka one on the strength of the patronymic alone. Further control is provided by the surviving parish registers of the Vladimirskaya church of Krasnoye for 1773, 1821, 1836, 1847 and 1850; of these, only the 1773 book has so far been found in the official list of digitised cases. Additional caution is needed because of a contradiction: the published census of 1716 reports the death of Miron's family, whereas the tale of 1722 shows it alive; both renderings should be checked against the originals. [PUB-ELETS-KRASNOE-ANPILOV-1722-1792]

A new check shows that the population of Trekhizbenskaya changed considerably even before Tymoshivka was founded. According to the description by Feodosy Makarevsky published in 1880, in 1795 many of the old residents left for the Kuban with the status of Cossacks, those who remained became odnodvortsy, and the vacated places were taken by people from Kursk and Voronezh governorates. The publication contains no list of names. In the primary scan of the revision of 1835, the whole small section of the Trekhizbenskaya odnodvortsy has been checked — ДАХО (State Archive of Kharkiv Oblast), f. 31, op. 141, d. 273, l. 24–24 v., families No. 92–99; the surname Anpilov/Anpilogov was not found as an explicit family name. This weakens the simple scenario of one household living there continuously, but does not prove that it left for Tymoshivka: the list is late and covers only one social estate. [PUB-MAKAREVSKY-TREKHIZBENSKAYA-MIGRATION-1795]
[PUB-DAHO-31-141-273-TREKHIZBENSKAYA-1835]

A detailed history of the resettlement gives a second date and a specific Kuban test. In 1801–1802 the odnodvortsy of Trekhizbenskaya were included in a general levy of inhabitants of eleven slobodas for the Caucasian Cordon Line; on 8 October 1802, 181 families settled in the new stanitsa (Cossack village) of Tiflisskaya. This may be a late stage of the process that Makarevsky dated to 1795, or an error in his late publication. The distribution of individual families has not yet been found, but the Kuban control has become much more precise. A parish register records an Anpilov in Tiflisskaya as early as 1804. V. A. Kolesnikov's alphabetical selection from the revision in ГАСК (State Archive of Stavropol Krai), f. 1209, op. 1, d. 1 shows exactly one household of the surname in 1815: in the note the sign `*` means that the surname is repeated, and Anpilovs do not have it. The primary scan of the confession list (ispovednaya rospis) of 1814 shows the widowed Taras Anpilov, 48, and his children Andrey, 20, Simeon, 17, Parfeny, 14, and Ulyana, 10. In the published list of 1827 this single Kuban household is named: No. 184, Taras Samoylovich Anpilov, widower. The agreement of name, widowhood, place and chronology almost certainly points to one man. This does not match the Tymoshivka group of Ilya Ioannovich and the four adult Ilyiches. In the Trekhizbenskaya household of 1762 that has been read there is no Samoil: Onisim, his wife and the sons Ivan and Andrey are named. So even the line Trekhizbenskaya → Tiflisskaya remains for now a resettlement corridor rather than a proved chain of generations. The open data noticeably weaken the route Trekhizbenskaya → Tymoshivka and support a separate line Trekhizbenskaya → Tiflisskaya. A late record of Ivan Ivanovich Anpilov, born in Tiflisskaya in 1898, shows the continuation of this Kuban surname.
[PUB-TREKHIZBENSKAYA-TO-KUBAN-TIFLISSKAYA-1802]
[PUB-TIFLISSKAYA-ANPILOV-1804-1827]

The neighbouring Belgorod–Kursk branch in Poroz in Khotmyzhsk district has also been checked. The primary scan of the revision of 1782 shows two Anpilov households — those of Nikita Kozmin and Pyotr Vasilyev — but no Ilya Ivanov syn. In 1850 six households of the surname still remained in Poroz. Poroz is therefore important as part of the old area, but the version that this branch resettled in full to Tymoshivka is weakened. To test a separate departure, the revision of Poroz of 1795 remains. [PUB-DAHO-31-141-33-POROZ-ANPILOV-1782]

The regional background agrees with this hypothesis: a complete check of version 36 of the open by-surname index of seventeenth-century service people yielded 63 surname lines and 111 total occurrences of the family series under study, without deduplicating people between documents. Most fall to Belgorod and Kursk — 19 each — followed by Oryol with 16, Khotmyzhsk/Khotmyshsk with 10, Livny with 8, Kromy with 7 and Novy Oskol with 6. The forms Anpilov/Anpilogov are also present in Oboyan and Sudzha in 1633–1701. This confirms the old area of the surname, but not the kinship of individual bearers. [PUB-SERVICE-PEOPLE-XVII-ANPILOGOV-FRONTIER-1633-1701]

Within the broad Kursk–Oryol area a new priority has emerged — Kromy district. The inspection lists (smotrennye spiski) of 1686–1689 name an Ilya there and numerous Anpilogovs, and the census in РГАДА, f. 1209, op. 1, part 1, d. 1040, for 1710 records at least ten surname entries of service people in Cheremoshnaya, Teterevinka, Lyutskoye, Dolgaya Lopteva and a neighbouring hamlet. The public Landmilitia Database adds 26 lines of the surname, mainly from Kromy and Kursk districts; among them Lavrenty Ilyin Anpilogov of Kromy district appears twice. The coincidence of the patronymic «Ильин» (Ilyin, "son of Ilya") with the adult Ilyiches of Tymoshivka is only a search marker: the year, regiment, settlement and the question of whether one entry is repeated have not yet been established. [PUB-RGADA-1209-1-1040-KROMY-ANPILOGOVY-1710]
[PUB-UKRAINIAN-LINE-LANDMILITIA-ANPILOGOV-KURSK-KROMY-1732-1754]

A repeat check of the full publication of the sorting book (razbornaya kniga) of 1686–1687 has produced a more precise layer: Karp, Ilya and the deceased Savin are recorded as Deyeviches, and the same book names a Dey Ivanov Anpilogov. A possible father–sons construction requires the original family formulas. The migration heading, on the other hand, is unambiguous: Averky Grigoryevich and Aleksey Ivanovich Anpilogov are placed among the people who «из Кромского уезда сошли» ("left Kromy district"), and the compiler of the book stated where they went and in what year. In the published alphabetical list these two fields are omitted. The two full lines have therefore become a separate short order: they will show the real mechanism and distance of the departure of Kromy Anpilogovs as early as the seventeenth century. These are not ancestors of the Tymoshivka family by default, nor proof of a route to Taurida.
[PUB-VGD-RGADA-1157-1-8-KROMY-ANPILOGOV-PATRONYMICS-1686-1687]

For a direct test of the Kromy version, an official leaf-level route has been found. ГАОО (State Archive of Oryol Oblast), f. 760, op. 1, d. 248 contains 692 leaves of the sixth revision of 1811 for the odnodvortsy and economic peasants of Kromy and its district. The internal register, read from the primary source, locates Cheremoshnaya on l. 196, the village (derevnya) of Lyutskaya on l. 200, the village (selo) of Lyutskoye on l. 216, and Teterevinka, called the village of Troitskoye, on l. 504. The next entries begin on ll. 200, 216, 252 and 516, so the full target block is ll. 196–251 v. and 504–515 v. The mark `[ВР]` means "internal register", not a virtual reading room; the public card shows only three pages of the register, not the tales themselves. On these leaves one must look for Ilya Ivanov Anpilogov, born about 1751, his sons Aleksey, Pavel, Ioann and Timofey, and a formula of departure to Taurida governorate. The control of the remaining households is provided by the seventh revision of 1816 — ГАОО, f. 760, op. 1, d. 251, 1,521 leaves. The already known petition of two Anpilogov families from Cheremoshnaya to resettle in Kherson governorate in 1811 proves the southward mobility of this branch, but does not by itself lead it to Tymoshivka. Moreover, the composition of the two households does not match the group being sought: Yakov Laktionovich had the children Varvara, Natalya, Maksim, Avksenty and Agafya, and Stefan Anikeyevich a son Andrey, a daughter-in-law Yevdokiya and a grandson Yakov. In the revision, therefore, other Kromy households must be sought, and these two used as a negative control. [PUB-GAOO-760-1-248-KROMY-1811-INTERNAL-REGISTER]
[RGIA-F1285-O1-D131-CHEREMOSHNAYA-ANPILOGOV-1811]

The Kromy version has gained an independent southern control. In the primary confession lists of Medvezhye in Stavropol district for 1812 and 1813, the 43-year-old Semyon Osipov syn Anpilov is recorded twice with his wife Anisiya Pimonova and four daughters. The headings on separate frames confirm that this is Medvezhye, not Pelagiada as first assumed. Immediately before his household stand Alifan Ledenev and Kondrat Derensky; both names are in the list of oxen and implements issued to the group of Stepan Pavlikov. According to an archival extract, this mixed group consisted of 32 families from Sychyovka district of Smolensk governorate, who arrived in 1805, and 22 families from Kromy and Karachev districts of Oryol governorate, who arrived in 1806. This proves one more early southern household of the surname and makes Kromy/Karachev a real competitor to the Belgorod version. But proximity in the list does not assign Semyon to either of the two parts of the party: that requires the complete list ГАСК 87-1-168 or a revision note about his former community. Semyon is not identified with Ilya Ivanovich of Tymoshivka and is not yet declared his relative.
[YA-GAAO-599-2-1577-MEDVEZHYE-SEMYON-OSIPOV-ANPILOV-1812-1813]

A search through the daughters' names led to the continuation of the Medvezhye household in 1824. The list expressly names «однодворца Семёна Анпилова» ("the odnodvorets Semyon Anpilov"), Anisiya, the 16-year-old Aleksey and the 13-year-old Agafya; next is again recorded Kondrat Derensky. Agafya continues the three-year-old girl of 1813 almost perfectly, and the repetition of a rare neighbour strengthens the identity. But the adults' ages diverge greatly, Anisiya is called Antonovna instead of Pimonovna, and Aleksey has not been read in the earlier household. The Record therefore registers a probable continuation with conflicts, not an undisputed merger.
[YA-GAAO-599-2-3684-MEDVEZHYE-SEMYON-ANPILOV-1824]

Another roundabout search has produced a fully proved route for an independent branch. In ГААО (State Archive of Astrakhan Oblast) case 687-8-12, the family of Ivan Anpilov resettled from the village of Babka in Pavlovsk district of Voronezh governorate to Sasykoli; in the title of the same case the representative is named in the rare form «Иван Антилов» (Ivan Antilov). The sons Mikhail, Ilya and Timofey then appear in the same order in the primary revision of Sasykoli of 1835, and the parish register entry of the birth of Darya to Mikhail Ivanov Anpilov independently checks the household. This is not the Tymoshivka Ilya: the family is younger and moved after Tymoshivka was founded. But the example proves the mechanism of a divided southern resettlement of Anpilovs and makes the revisions of Babka an addressable test for a possible older household.
[PUB-GAAO-687-8-12-BABKA-SASYKOLI-ANPILOV-MIGRATION-1827-1835]
[YA-GAAO-687-2-1365-SASYKOLI-ANPILOV-HOUSEHOLD-1835]

Continuing the search through Babka yielded a possible bridge across a generation. ## A new reverse test for Kromy

In Kromy district a separate small village of Anpilogovka has been discovered. Its supplementary revision tale of 1834–1840 occupies only four leaves: ГАОО, f. 760, op. 1, d. 264, ll. 345–348 v. This matters more than the mere similarity of the name: the inhabitants' previous numbers may lead directly to their households in the revision of 1816.

The primary internal register of 1850 calls it the `деревней Анпилоговой` ("village of Anpilogova") of the Slobodskoye community and counts 7 male and 7 female odnodvortsy. The official ГАОО reference book for 1858 again records an Anpilogovka of the same community and 6 male state peasants. So this was a tiny, stable settlement that can in practice be reconstructed in its entirety.

A new reverse control has located this offshoot settlement considerably more precisely. In the primary register of the sixth revision of 1811 there is as yet no separate Anpilogovka, but the village of Sloboda occupies a short block, ГАОО 760-1-248, ll. 484–495 v. In the census of 1710 it is precisely in the hamlet of Sloboda that the rare literal form `рейтар Антилогов` ("reitar Antilogov") is published — the same form of the surname appears independently in Tymoshivka for Aleksey and Timofey Ilyich.

The official printed table based on data of 1866 no longer contains a separate Anpilogovka. It names Malokhova (Sloboda), Starye Turyi and Chermoshnaya, and combines Mitasovka and Pyatnitskaya as variant names of the four-household Krapivka on the Svapa. The population of Malokhova (Sloboda) almost matches the village of Sloboda in the register of 1850, so the administrative community is now tied to a specific Turyi–Svapa cluster. The disappearance of a separate line for Anpilogovka between 1858 and 1866 is compatible with the absorption or renaming of the small offshoot, but the specific receiving settlement has not yet been proved. ## The documented family, 1891–1914

The marriage of Grigory Andreyevich Ampilogov and Yevdokiya Isidorovna Rykova took place on 7 October 1891. Records have been found of the births of eleven children: Pelageya (1892), Yevfimy (1894), Agafiya (1896), Yelena (1897), Feodor (1900), Aleksey (1903), Yekaterina (1905), Ioann (1907), Klavdiya (1909), Vladimir (1910) and Valentina (1914).

Within this sequence the surname forms `Анпилев` (Anpilev), `Анпилов` (Anpilov), `Анпилогов` (Anpilogov) and `Ампилогов` (Ampilogov) occur. The recurrence of the parents makes it possible to link the records into one family, but the spelling variants are kept with each source.

A namesake, Grigory Andreyevich, who married Irina Ivanovna Naidenova in 1884 at the age of 18, is kept separately. His age does not allow him to be automatically regarded as the same man.

### A precise source for checking the move Sorokino → Bor

What has been found is not yet another general list but a specialised book of departures: РГАДА, f. 350, op. 2, d. 3400, ll. 1–1166. According to the published archival description, it brings together the inhabitants of Stary Oskol and nine stans who left after the first revision; the book was compiled in 1748. If the report that some Anpilovs moved from Sorokino to Bor is based on a direct revision formula, this is the most likely place to look for it.

But for now this is the address of a source, not a surname line that has been found. The route is therefore not added to the map. The check should look not only for the surname, but for Demid, Ivan Demidovich born about 1717, and the collective destinations of departure of the neighbouring Sorokino households. The result must then be brought together with the complete Bor household of 1762, where an Ilya of about 11 is expected.
