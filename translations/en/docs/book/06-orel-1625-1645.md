# The Oryol Families: Children, Musters and the Oath

In 1625 a land extract (vypis) names the children of three Anfilogovs: Karp, Ivan and Vasily. A vypis was an excerpt copied from a large register book.

{{KINSHIP:orel-1625}}

## Familiar Names and Estates

Ivan's family is recorded at Belaya, Vasily's at Bazdyrevo: we have already seen these places in the book of 1594/95. Karp is most likely the same Karp Mikulin syn (Karp, son of Mikula) from the earlier entry. Thirty years had passed, and on the same land their children had grown up.

## Summer Service at Mtsensk

From spring to autumn, regiments stood at Mtsensk against Crimean raids. The servicemen of Oryol and six neighbouring towns served there in two shifts — «половинами» ("halves"). In April 1626 the Oryol man **Vasily Dmitriyevich Onpilogov** stood at the muster. From 1 July his half was relieved by **Ivan Vasilyevich**, **Aleksey Vasilyevich** and **Mark Karpovich Onfilogov**, and on 4 October, after the last inspection, they were dismissed to their homes.

The muster was the chief examination of a serviceman. Men were called by list, and each presented himself to the voivode — the military commander sent from Moscow: on what horse, with what weapons, with how many men of his own. Anyone who failed to appear was entered among the «нети» (absentees), and for not appearing his estate or pay could be cut.

## People of the Rebuilt Fortress

In the Time of Troubles — the years of anarchy and war at the beginning of the 17th century — Oryol was laid waste, and the fortress was rebuilt only in 1636. In 1641/42 an estimate book (smetnaya kniga) was compiled in the town — a list of the servicemen with their horses and weapons, that is, of those with whom Oryol would be defended. In it appear **Vasily Dmitriyevich Anpilov**, **Ivan Vasilyevich**, **Aleksey Vasilyevich**, **Afanasy Osipovich** and **Kuzma Karpovich Anfilogov**. For Vasily a gelding and a pishchal — a hand-held firearm — are noted.

## 1645: The Oath to the New Tsar

In the summer of 1645 Mikhail Fyodorovich, the first tsar of the Romanov dynasty, died. His sixteen-year-old son Aleksey ascended the throne, and the whole country swore allegiance to him. When swearing, people kissed the cross — hence the name **krestoprivodnaya** (cross-kissing oath) book, in which every Oryol man who took the oath was entered.

{{OREL_OATH_PRELUDE}}

In the book one can read Aleksey Vasilyevich and Kuzma Karpovich **Onfilogov**, Ivan Mikhaylovich **Anfilogov** and Anton Ivanovich **Onfilogov**. Grigory and Fyodor Dmitriyevich are recorded as **Anpiloga** — without the usual ending *-ov*, almost like the given name Anpilog itself.

How such a serviceman lived from one muster to the next is best seen in the records about Sergey Grigoryevich — first in Kursk, then in the new fortress of Oboyan.

**The 1625 extract.** Used on the basis of earlier research excerpts; the exact archival reference and images have not yet been brought into the project. The identification of Karp with Karp Mikulin syn is probable, but the original is needed to join the generations definitively. [S-OREL-1625]

**The 1626 Mtsensk muster.** РГАДА (Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts), f. 210, op. 12, d. 13: «Смотренный список смотра во Мценске дворян и детей боярских гг. Белёва, Болхова, Карачева, Мценска, Орла, Новосиля, Черни и белёвских бобриковских казаков» (Muster list of the muster at Mtsensk of the dvoryane and deti boyarskie of the towns of Belyov, Bolkhov, Karachev, Mtsensk, Oryol, Novosil, Chern and of the Bobrikovo Cossacks of Belyov). The musters were held by the voivodes Ivan Ivanovich Buturlin and Leonty Agafonovich Izvolsky; Vasily is recorded at the muster of 17 April and again on fol. 65. The readings are taken from a published line-by-line transcription; photocopies of the folios have not yet been obtained. Records: [Vasily, April](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L35) · [Ivan and Aleksey, July](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L105) · [Mark, July](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L106) · [Ivan and Aleksey, October](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L149) · [Mark, October](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L151). Ivan and Aleksey stand side by side in both lists, so the difference between their October forms cannot be explained by an edit of the whole list. Variants of the surname for the same people help to search for them under both forms, but they do not connect all namesakes from different districts. [S-OREL-1626]

**The 1641/42 estimate book.** РГАДА, f. 210, op. 4, d. 260; the book was compiled under the voivode Ivan Stepanovich Islenyev. The lines of Vasily, Ivan, Afanasy and Kuzma have been read from the original; Aleksey's name is confirmed by a preserved spread. The index also names Grigory and Fyodor Vasilyevich, but their lines have not yet been found on the working images. [S-OREL-1642]

**The 1645 oath.** РГАДА, f. 210, op. 6d, d. 54; six photographs have been checked: [Aleksey](/records/RGADA-210-6D-54-L1153OB) · [Kuzma](/records/RGADA-210-6D-54-L1155) · [Anton](/records/RGADA-210-6D-54-L1183). Grigory and Fyodor here are specifically **Dmitriyevich**: they must not be confused with the Vasilyevich brothers from the estimate book. [S-OREL-1645]
