# From Bazdyrevo to Shchigry: Akim's Story

In **1700** the servicemen of the town of Livny, south-east of Oryol, submitted **skazki** — written statements about their service, family and estates. They were collected by the Razryadny Prikaz (Military Service Office), the Moscow department that kept records of all the country's servicemen. Among the men of Livny was Akim Ivanovich Anpilogov, and his skazka is a rare case of a person of that time speaking about himself in his own words. And his story is a bitter one.

{{EVIDENCE_ROUTE:bazdyrevo}}

## A Lost Home

Akim was a syn boyarsky (a "boyar's son", a provincial serviceman) of Oryol and held an estate in Bazdyrevo — the very village where Anfilogov homesteads had stood back in 1594. According to him, the land was seized by force and without any lawful basis by the stolnik Mikhail Kosogov — a man of court rank, far above a provincial syn boyarsky. His house and property were ruined, and his peasant was taken away together with his wife and children.

Such things happened often at the end of the seventeenth century: noble and wealthy neighbours took over the lands of petty servicemen, and a lawsuit against the powerful could drag on for years.
## The Grandson's Land

Having lost the Oryol estate, the family found a foothold in Livny district — which is how Akim came to be among the Livny servicemen. Akim's grandson, **Tit Anpilogov**, held 30 chetverti there in the village of Dmitrievskoye, which was also called Shchigry: in the document, **«Щигры тож»** ("also Shchigry"). Later this Shchigry became the village of Vyazovoye — it is not the town of Shchigry in Kursk province.

{{KINSHIP:bazdyrevo}}

## Reiters and Lancers

Akim was not the only one to leave Oryol. The Kursk service skazki of 1697 name a whole [cluster of Anpilogovs](/records/PUB-SLUZHILYE-1697-OREL-KURSK-ANPILOGOV-MIGRATIONS) who had moved from Oryol and Kromy districts to Kursk district. Many of them no longer served the way their grandfathers had. From the 1630s, and especially in the second half of the seventeenth century, the Muscovite army was being reorganised along European lines: deti boyarskie were enrolled in regiments "of the new order" — reiter, lancer and dragoon regiments — where they were drilled and paid a salary. Reiters were heavy cavalry armed with pistols and carbines. [Ignat Guryevich Anpilogov](/records/PUB-RGADA-350-1-215-L1524-GURYEVICHI-1719), even in 1719, at the age of sixty-one, was still listed as an odnodvorets (single-household serviceman) «рейтарской службы» ("of reiter service").

Later another branch of Anfilogovs would gather old papers about their ancestors to prove their nobility.

**What exactly is said.** The village is called «Боздыревой» (Bozdyreva) — that is how the document renders Bazdyrevo. According to Akim, Kosogov «силою завладел насильством… без дачи» ("seized it by force and violence… without a grant"), that is, without any lawful transfer. This is the testimony of the injured party himself, not a court ruling. The name of the intervening generation — Tit's father — is not given; one must not insert there a person who merely fits by patronymic.

**Later traces.** In the revision (census) of 1782 a Vasily Dementyevich Anpilogov appears in this Shchigry; almost a century separates him from Tit, and the place name helps the search but does not fill in the generations. A secondary publication also mentions a skazka of 1697; the chapter's conclusions rest on the original of 1700, which has been read. [S-SHCHIGRY-1697] [S-SHCHIGRY-REVISIONS]

**Kursk Anpilogovs of 1697.** An open database of service skazki names the sons of Guriy, descendants of the Oryol servicemen Foma, Nikifor, Efim and others, and a separate Kromy line. Ivan Fomich's father served from Oryol as a reiter, Anikey Nikiforovich's father as a lancer; Terenty Guryevich dated the move to Kursk district to 1691/92. A link between these families and Akim has not been established.
