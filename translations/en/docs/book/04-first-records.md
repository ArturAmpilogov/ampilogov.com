# The First Records: a Name, a Household and a Village

The earliest verified record of an **Onfilogov** takes up a single line. In 1536/37 a census-taker went round the monastery village of Priluki on the Volga — such inventories were drawn up to know how much tax to collect from each household — and named the inhabitant of a non-ploughing household: **«Тонкой Онфилогов» (Tonkoy Onfilogov)**.

Peasants at that time had no surnames in today's sense. A person was recorded by a given name or nickname and by the father's name. Tonkoy's neighbours in the same line are Nekrasko Danilov and Khalya Zalogin: again a nickname plus the father's name. Such names changed with every generation, and only over time did one of them become fixed to a family.

## Forty Years Earlier

Around 1495–1497 a Yaroslavl monastery went to law over a hayfield on the bank of the Kotorosl, a tributary of the Volga. The case was heard by a tiun — a judge in the prince's service. Trials were then held in the presence of local men who could later confirm what had happened, and the charter lists them by name. The last one named is **[Anfilof Selivanov](/records/PUB-ASEI3-1495-YAROSLAVL-N208-ANFILOF)** — a living man with a close form of our name. "Selivanov" here most likely means simply "son of Selivan".

## A Non-Ploughing Household in Priluki

Priluki is a large village on the Volga between Uglich and Kashin. It was owned by the Trinity-Sergius Monastery near Moscow — by the sixteenth century one of the richest landowners in the country, whose villages were scattered far from its walls. In Priluki people traded in livestock and caught fish, and the scribes divided households into ploughing and non-ploughing: some lived off the land, others by other means.

**Tonkoy** is not a church name but rather a nickname, probably from the word «тонкий» (thin). **Onfilogov** means "son of Onfilog": so a generation earlier there lived a man whose name had grown out of Amfilokhiy.

## Onfilogovo: Four Households

A cadastral survey book (pistsovaya kniga) is an inventory of land: scribes sent from Moscow recorded villages, households, ploughland and hayfields in order to know who owed how much in taxes and service. In the Tver cadastral survey book of 1539/40, the village of Nikolskoye Svechino with its hamlets is recorded under Stefanida, wife of Ivan Yurlov — that is, as her holding. One of these hamlets is **[Onfilogovo](/records/PUB-PKMG-1540-TVER-P111-ONFILOGOVO)**, four households. Villages were often named after their first settler or owner: evidently a man called Onfilog once lived here. This is the earliest village bearing our name found so far.

## A Name Descendants Could Be Called After

Half a century later, far to the south, in the new frontier town of Oryol — the subject of the next chapter — the cadastral survey book of 1594/95 mentions **[Anpilog Ivanovich Onanyin](/records/PUB-PKOU-1594-P948-ANPILOG)**: by then his estate had passed to his grandson Kondratiy. Here Anpilog is simply the given name of a man from another family, but it shows that the name was in use.

And the same Oryol book already records a whole group of landholders with the surnames **Onfilogov**, **Anfilogov** and **Anpilogov**.

{{RESEARCH_NOTES}}

**Priluki, 1536/37.** The record survives as an extract from a sixteenth-century manuscript copy, published by the historian S. M. Kashtanov («По следам троицких копийных книг XVI в.», "On the Trail of the Trinity Monastery Copy Books of the 16th Century"). The abbreviation «в.» before a name stands for «во дворе» ("in the household"). The date 1536/37 covers a year running from September to August by the reckoning of the time. The document does not say whether Tonkoy's children inherited the name or how he made his living; kinship with the servitor families of Oryol and Kursk has not been established. [A study of the monastery's economy](https://www.booksite.ru/fulltext/che/rca/so/va/5.htm). [S-UGLICH-1536] [S-PRILUKI-HISTORY]

**Anfilof Selivanov.** A judgment charter (pravaya gramota) in the dispute of the Spaso-Yaroslavsky (Saviour) Monastery over the Krestsy hay meadow; the case was heard by the tiun Afanasy Durov. Anfilof's age and occupation are not given, and his kinship with the later Ampilogovs has not been established.

**Onfilogovo.** A hamlet in Khorvach volost of the Yuryev stan of Tver uyezd: 4 households, 30 chetverts of ploughland per field, 30 kopnas (stacks) of hay. The eponym's name is not given in the book and is reconstructed only from the place name; whether he was its owner or founder is unknown. A connection between the hamlet and Priluki (about 200 km away) has not been proven. [S-TVER-1540]

**Anpilog Onanyin.** The chain Anpilog — Vavil — Kondratiy belongs to the Onanyins; their kinship with Anfilogovs in the same book has not been established. [S-OREL-1594-ANPILOG]
