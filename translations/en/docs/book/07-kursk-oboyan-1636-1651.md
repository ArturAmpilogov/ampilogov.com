# Kursk and Oboyan: A Gelding, a Harquebus and Two Sons

In 1636 the Kursk serviceman Sergey Grigoryevich Onpilogov was required to serve «на меринке с пищалью» ("on a little gelding with a pishchal"), that is, on horseback, with a firearm of the period. In 1640 he turned up on foot with a *rogatina*, a heavy spear. In 1651 Sergey Grigoryevich Anpilogov is recorded in Oboyan, once again with a horse and a firearm. Most likely, the three lists show different years of one and the same life.

![A serviceman with a campaign horse and a pishchal by a southern fortress](/artwork/oboyan-service-man-1651.png "Artistic reconstruction. Sergey Grigoryevich's appearance is unknown.")

## A Land Allotment and Real Weapons

**Desyatni** (muster rolls) were drawn up at reviews and at the distribution of pay: they recorded who had come, how he was armed and what provision was due to him. The Kursk muster roll of 1636 assigns Sergey 150 *chetverts* of land, on paper; the actual holding was usually much smaller.

For a serviceman, a horse was both his chief wealth and his chief worry. The horse, saddle, pishchal, gunpowder and food for the whole summer were all bought at his own expense. A poor harvest, the loss of livestock or debts could easily turn a mounted warrior into a foot soldier.
## Five Roubles for a Life by the New Fortress

In the 1630s–1640s the frontier moved south again. Crimean raiding parties rode into Rus' along the steppe roads, the *shlyakhs*, and the state decided to block them with a continuous line of fortresses and *zaseki* (abatis barriers of felled trees). In those same years, in 1639, Oboyan was built on the Psyol River, sixty kilometres south of Kursk. One had to live in such fortresses permanently, not merely come for a tour of duty.

In the Oboyan muster roll of 1651 Sergey received five roubles in pay and undertook to live in the fortress: to build a homestead, plough the land, and keep a horse and weapons.

## Cossacks of the New Line

The new fortresses were not settled by *deti boyarskiye* (lesser gentry servitors) alone. Alongside them served men recruited «по прибору» ("by enlistment"): Cossacks, *streltsy* (musketeers), gunners. They were not entered into service by right of their fathers but recruited from free men and given a homestead, arable land and pay. In July 1637, 105 free men from various towns were recruited as regimental mounted Cossacks for Userd, another fortress of the new line. Among the first settlers were [Anton Sofonovich and Pyotr Gavrilovich Onpilogov](/records/RGADA-210-12-85-L131-134-USERD-ONPILOGOVY): each was given 8 roubles to build a homestead, a pishchal, gunpowder and lead, a plot for a homestead, arable land and a hayfield.

## A Family in a Single Line

In the Oboyan muster roll, next to Sergey, his sons are recorded: **Sidor, aged 20**, and **Yeremey, aged 10**. At Sidor's age the sons of servicemen were usually already entered into service: the family was raising its own replacements.

But even life «на вечном житье» ("in permanent residence") did not protect a household from powerful neighbours. Another serviceman, Akim Anpilogov, left an account of how he lost his estate. His story takes us back to Bazdyrevo, familiar from the Oryol cadastral book.

**The Kursk muster roll of 1636.** Review of 18 March 1636, RGADA (Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts), f. 210, op. 4, d. 215; Sergey — l. 103 ob. (fol. 103v). The entry is recorded for now as a lead: a photocopy of the folio has yet to be obtained. The [1640 review](/records/PUB-KURKULEV-KURSK-1640-ONPILOGOVY) keeps the same allotment. [S-KURSK-1636]

**Other Kursk bearers of the surname.** Recorded earlier than Sergey is Fyodor Grigoryevich Onpilogov; other lists name men called Semyon and Vasily Semyonovich. The shared patronymic makes it possible that Fyodor and Sergey were brothers, but no source calls them brothers. [Kursk list of the early 1630s](/records/PUB-KURKULEV-KURSK-1632-ONPILOGOVY).

**The Oboyan muster roll of 1651.** RGADA, f. 210, op. 4, d. 228, ll. 39 ob.–40 (fols. 39v–40); published by I. P. Babin and M. M. Ozerov (2017). [Sergey's entry](/records/PUB-OBOYAN-1651-L39OB-SERGEY). The approximate years of birth of the sons are 1631 and 1641; Sergey's own age is not given. [S-OBOYAN-1651]

**Kursk to Oboyan.** The first name, patronymic and rare surname link the Oboyan Sergey with the Kursk one, but there is no separate document recording a transfer: the path remains probable. A family relationship between Sergey and Akim Anpilogov has not been established.

**Userd, 1637.** Register of newly enlisted regimental Cossacks: RGADA, f. 210, op. 12, d. 85, ll. 131–134. The previous towns of Anton and Pyotr, their relationship to each other and to Kursk Onpilogovs have not been established.
