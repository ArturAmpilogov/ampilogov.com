# The Nobility of One Anfilogov Branch

One branch of Anfilogovs was entered in **Part VI of the genealogical book of Kursk province** — the section for ancient noble families. A pre-revolutionary encyclopedia of 1911 named as the family's founder Vasily Dmitrievich Anfilogov, who served from Oryol.

{{SOURCE_EXCERPT:nes-anfilogovy}}

## Why Prove the Antiquity of a Family

Under Catherine II the nobility became an estate with clearly defined rights. Nobles were not conscripted as recruits, they did not pay the poll tax — a tax levied on every male soul — they could not be subjected to corporal punishment, and only they could own estates with serfs. These rights were secured by an entry in the genealogical book of one's province.

The book was divided into six parts, and the sixth was considered the most honourable: it recorded families whose nobility reached back into the depths of the centuries. One had to prove that one's ancestors had belonged to the nobility for more than a hundred years — the actual origin of the family could remain unknown. For descendants of seventeenth-century servicemen, the evidence for this came from the old military service registers — the very muster books (smotrennye knigi) and desyatni (service rolls) we saw in earlier chapters.

## An Ancestor in a Service List

In the Oryol muster record of 1626 stands **«Васка Дмитреев сын Онпилогов»** (Vaska, son of Dmitry, Onpilogov) — the same Vasily we saw on summer service at Mtsensk. Name, patronymic, town and period match the encyclopedia's information. How a pedigree grew out of this line should be shown by the file of the Heraldry Office (Geroldiya) — the department that verified proofs of nobility.

## Different Fates from the Same Service

Only one branch became nobles. The descendants of other service families turned into odnodvortsy (single-household servitors), and later into peasants and townspeople (meshchane). Meanwhile, the surname also remained on the land itself — in the names of villages.

{{RESEARCH_NOTES}}

**One family, not the whole surname.** Noble status applies to a particular branch of Anfilogovs, not to every bearer of the surname.

**The encyclopedia.** The «Новый энциклопедический словарь» (New Encyclopedic Dictionary) of Brockhaus and Efron, edited by K. K. Arsenyev, vol. 3 (1911), col. 108: Anfilogovs are called a Russian noble family, with Vasily Dmitrievich as its founder; the family is entered in Part VI of the Kursk genealogical book. The article gives no intermediate generations. [S-NOBILITY-NES]

**The law.** The rule on Part VI is article 82 of the Charter to the Nobility of 1785: families with proofs of nobility going back more than a hundred years counted as ancient. [S-NOBILITY-LAW]

**Vasily Dmitrievich.** [The April muster of 1626](/records/RGADA-210-12-13-L35). The match with the founder named in the encyclopedia needs to be checked against the Heraldry Office file; so far only its title and dates are known.

**Files in RGIA.** The Heraldry Office files are held in RGIA (Russian State Historical Archive) in St Petersburg. Digital images of files 2345 and 2348 are not in the open catalogue, and the generational pedigree has not yet been extracted from them. [S-NOBILITY-RGIA]
