# R0013 — Vasily Dmitriyevich Anpilov, 1641/42

## Original spelling

> Василей Дмитреев сын Анпилов на мерине и с пищалью.

> *Translation:* Vasiley, son of Dmitry, Anpilov, on a gelding and with a pishchal (matchlock gun).

## Full transcription

Василей Дмитреев сын Анпилов на мерине и с пищалью.

*Translation:* Vasiley, son of Dmitry, Anpilov, on a gelding and with a pishchal.

This is the whole of the independent entry: the name, patronymic and surname form occupy the first line, and the information on horse and weapon continues on the next two lines.

## Extracted facts

- name: Vasiley;
- patronymic in the source: Dmitreyev syn ("son of Dmitry");
- surname form: Anpilov;
- a gelding is recorded at the muster;
- weapon: pishchal;
- the entry is in the Oryol estimate book (smetnaya kniga) of 7150.

## Service and muster context

The Oryol part of the file is the estimate book of 7150, compiled under the Oryol voivode **Ivan Stepanovich Islenyev**. The printed inventory gives him only by the initials «И. С.» ("I. S."), but biographical and genealogical reference works assign the Oryol voivodeship of 1641 precisely to Ivan Stepanovich Islenyev. The earlier expansion of the patronymic as "Semyonovich" was mistaken.

The estimate book was not a list of a single army that had set out on campaign, but a local check of the state of a service town. The voivode's administration recorded which servicemen were present, on what horse and with what weapon each could serve, as well as the state of the fortress, artillery and stores. So the words «на мерине и с пищалью» ("on a gelding and with a pishchal") are a brief assessment of Vasily's combat readiness.

This entry does not assign him to march anywhere from Oryol: no regiment, campaign assembly point or direction of service is named here. Nor can Vasily be called a novik. The line contains no formula of first enrolment, no pomestye or cash oklad and not the word «новик» itself; he is recorded as a serviceman already present. A possible identity with the Vasily Dmitriyevich of the documents of the 1620s would make novik status even less likely, but that identity has not yet been proven.

Historically this is a muster of the garrison of a recently restored fortress. Oryol was rebuilt in 1636 to defend the southern frontier against Crimean and Nogai raids. The estimate of 1641/42 records the town's readiness a few years after its restoration, when raids on the southern frontier continued.

## What it proves

- the existence of Vasily Dmitriyevich Anpilov in the Oryol estimate book of 7150;
- the documentary surname form `Анпилов`;
- the horse and pishchal stated in the entry.

## What it does not prove

- that the scribe mistakenly shortened the surname `Анпилогов` to `Анпилов`;
- identity with the man of the same name in the Oryol lists of the 1620s;
- kinship with other Anpilovs, Anpilogovs or Onpilogovs.
- the voivode Islenyev's personal involvement in recording this particular line: the inventory names the responsible voivode, but not the scribe or the immediate inspector;
- Vasily's assignment to a particular campaign or regiment;
- novik status or first enrolment in service.
