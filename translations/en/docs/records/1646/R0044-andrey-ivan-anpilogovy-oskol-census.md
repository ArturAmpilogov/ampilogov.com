# R0044 — The household of Andrey Petrovich Anpilogov and his son Ivan, 16 December 1646

## Original spelling

> Двор — крестьянин Андрюшка Петров сын Анпилогов, у него сын Ивашко.

> *Translation:* A household — the peasant Andryushka, son of Pyotr, Anpilogov; he has a son, Ivashko.

## Family and household

The census directly records one household and two of its inhabitants:

- **Andryushka Petrov syn Anpilogov** ("Andryushka, son of Pyotr, Anpilogov") — head of the household, a monastery peasant;
- **Ivashko** — his son, that is, Ivan Andreyevich.

The son's surname is not repeated separately. In the card it is restored as the family surname on the direct statement about the father, not as a literal reading of a second surname line.

## Who conducted the census

By decree of Tsar Aleksey Mikhaylovich the census and in-person inspection of households were carried out by **Afanasy Fyodorovich Bobarykin** and the clerk **Ivan Gavrilov**. They worked in Oskol, in its posad (trading quarter), in the slobody (settlements), on church lands and across the pomestye and votchina (service and hereditary) estates of Oskol district.

## What exactly was checked

The compilers did not confine themselves to the landholders' earlier declarations. The preamble says that they recorded people **«сверх их прежних сказок»** ("beyond their earlier declarations") and inspected in person:

- peasant and bobyl (landless cottager) households;
- the peasants and bobyli themselves;
- their children, brothers and nephews;
- sosedi and podsosedniki (co-residents and lodgers).

The Anpilogov household is part of the block held by the St Nicholas Monastery on the Kholkovo gorodishche (hillfort site). In the publication's totals, the households and people newly found beyond the monastery's earlier declaration are listed. This allows us to say that the household was discovered during the supplementary check. But Andrey in particular cannot be accused of deliberate concealment: the earlier declaration was submitted by the monastery administration, and the reason a given household was omitted is unknown.

## Social status

Andrey is a **monastery peasant**, and Ivan is his son in the same household. This is an important contrast with the numerous Anpilogovs in state service: the same surname form existed in the dependent peasant milieu too.

The phrase «за Никольским монастырём» ("held by the St Nicholas Monastery") means that the household belonged to the monastery. Kholkovo gorodishche is the site of the monastery in Orlinsky stan of Oskol district.

## Was this a military review

No. Bobarykin and Gavrilov were conducting a household census of the population, not an inspection of fighting men. Andrey and Ivan are not called deti boyarskie, noviki or servicemen. The document has no horses, weapons, oklads, assembly point or campaign direction.

## Historical context

The censuses of 1646 were connected with the state's binding of the tax-paying population and the checking of who owned them. For the southern districts, with their mobile population, the in-person check of households was especially important: the scribes compared the actual inhabitants with what landholders and monasteries had previously reported.

## What it proves

- the direct father–son link between Andrey and Ivan;
- that they lived together in one household;
- the peasant and monastic status of the household;
- that the family was in Orlinsky stan of Oskol district by December 1646.

## What it does not prove

- Ivan's age or the existence of other family members;
- the family's origin before 1646;
- a blood link with Anpilogovs in state service in Belgorod, Kursk or Oryol;
- military service;
- deliberate concealment of the household by Andrey himself.

## What to look for next

An image of fol. 91v of the RGADA book is needed. For tracing the family further, the later censuses of Oskol district and the monastery books are especially important, where Ivan might appear with a household of his own.
