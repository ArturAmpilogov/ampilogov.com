# R0048 — Anpilog Ivanovich Onanyin, Former Holder of an Oryol Pomestye

## Original spelling

> …что было за дѣдомъ его за Анпилогомъ (sic) за Ивановымъ сыномъ Онаньина въ тойже дер. на Государнинѣ селищѣ, на р. на Орлѣ, подъ Корчаковымъ лѣсомъ…

> *Translation:* …which had been held by his grandfather Anpilog (sic), son of Ivan, Onanyin, in the same hamlet on the Gosudarnino selishche, on the river Orel, below the Korchakov forest…

## The key distinction: a given name, not a surname

Here **Анпилог** (Anpilog) is the man's personal name, and his family designation is **Онаньин** (Onanyin). The full modern rendering is **Anpilog Ivanovich Onanyin**.

This is a direct documentary link between the baptismal name Amphilochius (Amfilokhy) and the later surname:

`Амфилохий → разговорное Анпилог → сын или потомок Анпилога → Анпилогов`.

On the available evidence, Anpilog Onanyin himself does not belong to the Anpilogov family. He matters as a real person who bore the name from which such a surname could have been formed.

## Why the entry is retrospective

In 1594/1595 the pomestye was already held by Anpilog's grandson, Kondraty Vavilovich Onanyin. The scribe used the formula `что было за дедом его` ("which had been held by his grandfather"), preserving the name of the former holder. It follows that:

- Anpilog was Kondraty's grandfather;
- between them there was an intermediate generation — Kondraty's father Vavila;
- this line contains no separate statement about Vavila: his name is reconstructed from the patronymic `Вавилов сын` ("son of Vavila");
- Anpilog held the zherebye (land share) before 1594/1595, but no exact dates are given.

The estimate of birth around 1500–1535 is only a genealogical calculation over two generations back from an adult grandson. It is not a date from the document.

## The pomestye

The zherebye lay in a hamlet on the Gosudarnino selishche, on the river Orel, below the Korchakov forest. This was a multi-household servicemen's settlement of Korchakov stan. The later entry on the grandson describes on the share a landholder's household, 7 chetverti of ploughed land, 44 chetverti of wild field in a field and 110 kopny of hay. It cannot be assumed automatically that all of this existed unchanged in the grandfather's time.

## Who recorded the information

The cadastral book was compiled by the scribe **Dementy Yakovlev** and the clerk (podyachy) **Leonty Sofonov** under Tsar Fyodor Ivanovich. They recorded present and former holders in order to verify the succession of pomestye grants.

## The note `(sic)`

The editor of the 1877 publication placed the note `(sic)` after the name `Анпилогомъ`, that is, he flagged the unusual form and rendered it literally. This adds to the value of the reading: the name is not a modern reconstruction by the editor. A final palaeographic check, however, still requires the РГАДА manuscript.

## Service, novik status and campaigns

The context of Korchakov stan is that of pomestya of Oryol deti boyarskie of various services: regimental, stanitsa, samopal and siege men. So Anpilog was a service landholder. But the entry does not name his particular service category.

This is not a military muster. The document does not say who inspected his horse and weapons, where the army was assembled, or whether Anpilog was ever a novik (newly enrolled serviceman).

## What it proves

- the existence of the personal name `Анпилог` in Oryol district;
- the patronymic Ivanovich and the surname Onanyin;
- a direct grandfather–grandson relationship between Anpilog and Kondraty;
- former possession of the same pomestye share.

## What it does not prove

- kinship between the Onanyins and Anfilogovs;
- the exact years of Anpilog's life;
- the name of his wife or the full composition of the family;
- any particular military service or campaigns;
- that the size of the pomestye was unchanged since the grandfather's time.
