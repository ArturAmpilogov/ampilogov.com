# R0053 — Ivan Pershin Anfilogov in the Hamlet of Belaya, 1594/1595

## Full entry

> За Иваномъ за Першиньмъ сыномъ Анфилогова въ той же дер. въ Бѣлой, на р. на Оцнѣ, подъ Миновымъ лѣсомъ, на его жеребью: дв. помѣщиковъ; пашни паханые добр. земли 4 чети, да дикого поля 29 чети въ полѣ, а въ дву потому жъ, сѣна 50 коп.

> *Translation:* Held by Ivan, son of Persha, Anfilogov, in the same hamlet of Belaya, on the river Otsna, below the Minov forest, on his share: a landholder's household; ploughed land of good soil 4 chetverti, and wild field 29 chetverti in a field, and in the other two likewise; hay 50 kopny.

## Name and surname

The scribe called the holder **Ivan, son of Persha, Anfilogov** (Иван Першин сын Анфилогова). `Першин` here means "son of Persha": Persha is an independent Old Russian secular name, not a second surname. The standard modern form of a patronymic from this name is debatable, so the main name keeps the form **Ivan Pershin Anfilogov**. The variant `Першич` (Pershich) found in later reference literature does not replace the literal reading of the book.

The surname is printed as `Анфилогова`. On neighbouring pages of the same book the forms `Онфилогова` and `Анпилогова` occur; Ivan's own entry gives no grounds for correcting his surname to the modern `Ампилогов`.

## Household and land

Recorded under Ivan:

- his own landholder's household;
- 4 chetverti of ploughed land of good soil;
- 29 chetverti of wild field in each field of the three-field system;
- a hay meadow of 50 kopny.

The ploughed part was small compared with the land not yet brought under cultivation. This is typical of the young Oryol frontier: the fortress of Oryol was built in 1566, and less than thirty years had passed by the time of the survey. The book does not, however, say when exactly Ivan received the share or whether he was the first to plough it.

## The hamlet of Belaya and a possible continuation of the family

Belaya stood on the river Otsna below the Minov forest. Below the same forest lay Bazdyrevo, where Belyay, Osip and the three sons of Mikula lived, with variants of the same surname. This is not a random scattering across the district but a tight territorial cluster in Korchakov stan.

In an extract (vypis) of 1625 an **Ivan Anfilogov** is again named in Belaya, now with sons Ilya, Vasily and Anton. The coincidence of name, surname and pomestye hamlet makes the identification with the Ivan Pershin of 1594/95 a strong working hypothesis. But the later extract does not call Ivan `Першиным` ("son of Persha"), so the entries cannot yet be merged into one person without an intermediate book.

## Who carried out the survey

The pomestya were measured by the sovereign's scribe **Dementy Yakovlev** and the clerk **Leonty Sofonov** under Tsar Fyodor Ivanovich. They travelled through the stany, recording holders, households, ploughland, wild field and hay meadows. This is a land survey, not a personal muster of servicemen.

## Service, novik status and campaigns

Ivan belonged to the Oryol pomestye corporation of deti boyarskie. The general heading of the stan covers regimental, stanitsa, samopal and siege men, but the individual entry does not name his category. Ivan is not called a novik; horse, weapons and cash and land oklady are not given here.

The book did not assemble an army for a campaign and did not assign regiments. So this entry has no assembly point, no voivode conducting a muster and no direction of service. It shows possession of land, but not Ivan's part in any specific campaign.

## What it proves

- that Ivan held a share and a landholder's household in Belaya;
- the father's name Persha and the surname form `Анфилогова`;
- the size of the ploughland, wild field and hay meadow;
- the presence of the surname in the same forest area as the Bazdyrevo group.

## What it does not prove

- identity with the Ivan Anfilogov of the 1625 extract;
- the names of his wife and children;
- any particular kind of military service, weapons or campaign;
- the family's origin before the founding of Oryol.
