# R0055 — Ivan Nechayevich Anpilogov Takes Up a Zaimishche on the Radomlya, 1594/1595

## Full entry

> За Иваномъ за Нечаевымъ сыномъ Анпилогова займище на ручк. на Радомлѣ съ правую сторону, противъ Захара Нечаева сына Гревцова съ товарищи; пашни дикого поля добр. земли 15 чети въ полѣ, а въ дву потому жъ, сѣна 80 коп., лѣсъ дуброва.

> *Translation:* Held by Ivan, son of Nechay, Anpilogov: a zaimishche on the brook Radomlya, on the right side, opposite Zakhar, son of Nechay, Grevtsov and his fellows; ploughland of wild field of good soil 15 chetverti in a field, and in the other two likewise; hay 80 kopny; woodland, oak grove.

## Not a ready-made hamlet but a new zaimishche

Ivan's entry is sharply different from those of the neighbouring landholders. No landholder's or peasant household is named for him, and there is no ploughed land. All that is recorded is:

- 15 chetverti of good wild field in each field of a future three-field system;
- a hay meadow of 80 kopny;
- oak woodland;
- a position on the right side of the Radomlya brook, opposite the holding of Zakhar Nechayevich Grevtsov and his fellows.

A `займище` (zaimishche) is a plot taken up for new cultivation. What we have here is not yet an established hamlet but a pomestye clearing, where a household was still to be built, the oak grove cleared and the ploughland broken.

## A seven-year exemption

In the summary section of the book Ivan is counted among the lgotchiki (holders with a tax exemption). The exemption began on 20 January 7103 and was granted for seven years — until 20 January 7110, that is, until 20 January 1602. It freed the new ploughland from part of the tax burden while it was being developed.

The brief name line on page 1011 itself does not repeat the term: it is reconstructed from the general total of the cadastral book. So the exemption reliably applies to Ivan's holding, but should not be presented as a separate personal charter issued to him on this folio.

## The name Nechay and the neighbour Grevtsov

`Нечаев` means "son of Nechay". Nechay is an Old Russian secular name, so the modern rendering of the holder's name is **Ivan Nechayevich Anpilogov**. Opposite him is named Zakhar, son of Nechay, Grevtsov. The same father's name may point to two sons of one Nechay, but their surnames differ and the source states no kinship. Without a further entry Ivan and Zakhar cannot be considered brothers.

## Who carried out the survey

The zaimishche was measured by the scribe **Dementy Yakovlev** and the clerk **Leonty Sofonov**. For the state it was important to record not only ploughland already in use but also the future tax base: the area of wild field, hay meadow, woodland and the term of the exemption for development.

This is not a muster of fighting men. The scribes noted the land and its tax regime, not the holder's horse, weapons or fitness for service.

## Service, novik status and campaigns

Ivan was a pomestye holder from the Oryol service milieu, but not a `новик` (novik) in the terms of this entry. The newness of the zaimishche is not the same as a man's first enrolment in service. His age, earlier holdings and the year he began service are unknown.

The book names no army assembly or campaign direction. The seven-year exemption concerns the development of land on the frontier, not release from all military service.

## What it proves

- that Ivan held a zaimishche on the Radomlya;
- the surname form `Анпилогова` and the father's name Nechay;
- the absence of any recorded household or ploughed land;
- the presence of wild field, hay meadow and oak grove;
- the exemption regime for development, according to the general total of the book.

## What it does not prove

- that Ivan was a new serviceman;
- kinship with Zakhar Nechayevich Grevtsov;
- the existence of an established hamlet on this site in 1594/95;
- participation in any particular muster, regiment or campaign.
