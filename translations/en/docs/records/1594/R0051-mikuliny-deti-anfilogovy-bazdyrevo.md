# R0051 — Vasily, Timofey and Karp, Sons of Mikula, Anfilogovs, 1594/1595

## Full entry

> За Васильемъ, да за Тимофѣемъ, да за Карпомъ за Микулиными дѣтми Анфилогова въ той же дер. въ Баздыревѣ, на Сычовѣ колодезѣ, подъ Миновымъ лѣсомъ, на ихъ жеребью: дв. помѣщиковъ Васильевъ, дв. помѣщиковъ Тимофѣевъ, дв. помѣщиковъ Карповъ; пашни паханые добр. земли 6 чети, да дикого поля 51 четь въ полѣ, а въ дву потому жъ, сѣна 115 коп.

> *Translation:* Held by Vasily, and Timofey, and Karp, the children of Mikula, Anfilogovs, in the same hamlet of Bazdyrevo, on the Sychov spring, below the Minov forest, on their share: Vasily's landholder's household, Timofey's landholder's household, Karp's landholder's household; ploughed land of good soil 6 chetverti, and wild field 51 chetverti in a field, and in the other two likewise; hay 115 kopny.

## Three brothers and three households

A single entry explicitly names three **children of Mikula**:

- Vasily;
- Timofey;
- Karp.

The grammatical construction and the assignment of households by name show that these are the sons of one Mikula and therefore full brothers. The scribe separately noted `двор Васильев`, `двор Тимофеев`, `двор Карпов` (Vasily's, Timofey's and Karp's household): they held a joint share, but each had his own landholder's household.

The form `Микулович` (Mikulovich) in the cards is a modern rendering of the patronymic. Literally, the source calls them `Микулиными детьми` ("the children of Mikula").

## A joint land complex

Recorded for the three together:

- three landholders' households;
- 6 chetverti of ploughed land of good soil;
- 51 chetverti of wild field in a field, with the same area in each of the other two fields;
- 115 kopny of hay.

The book does not divide the ploughland and hay meadow between the brothers. The figures must not be split equally: the shares may have been unequal, and the entry describes a joint share.

## The father, Mikula

The father's name is reliably reconstructed as **Mikula**. But this entry contains no separate statement about his life, holding or service. So Mikula can be used as a generation in the pedigree, but without a separate source he cannot be assigned dates, a rank or this same pomestye.

## A possible next generation

The same book contains **Sofon, son of Timofey, Anfilogov**. The coincidence of a rare surname, the father's name Timofey and nearby territory makes the chain `Микула → Тимофей → Софон` quite likely. But Sofon's entry does not say that his father is this particular Timofey, son of Mikula, of Bazdyrevo. Until a further document is found this is a hypothesis, not an established link.

## Who carried out the survey

The scribe **Dementy Yakovlev** and the clerk **Leonty Sofonov** described and measured the pomestya of Oryol district. They recorded a level of detail rare for this book — three separate brothers' households within one joint grant.

## Service and military context

The brothers were landholders from among the Oryol deti boyarskie. But the entry does not assign them to regimental, stanitsa, samopal or siege service. This is not a muster, not a verstanie (enrolment) and not a campaign roster. The brothers are not called noviki; there is no information on horses, weapons or assembly point.

## What it proves

- the existence of three brothers, sons of Mikula;
- a separate landholder's household for each brother;
- joint possession of a share in Bazdyrevo;
- the surname form `Анфилогова`.

## What it does not prove

- an equal division of the land;
- the lifetime and service of the father, Mikula;
- that Sofon Timofeyevich was the son of this particular Timofey;
- a specific military category or the brothers' part in a campaign.
