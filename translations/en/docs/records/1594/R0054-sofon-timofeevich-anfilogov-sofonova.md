# R0054 — Sofon Timofeyevich Anfilogov and the Hamlet of Sofonova, 1594/1595

## Full entry

> За Софономъ за Тимофѣевымъ сыномъ Анфилогова дер. Софонова, подъ Черемошенскимъ лѣскомъ, а въ ней дв. помѣщиковъ; пашни паханые добр. земли 4 чети, да дикого поля 36 чети въ полѣ, а въ дву потому жъ, сѣна 85 коп.

> *Translation:* Held by Sofon, son of Timofey, Anfilogov: the hamlet of Sofonova, below the Cheremoshensky grove, and in it a landholder's household; ploughed land of good soil 4 chetverti, and wild field 36 chetverti in a field, and in the other two likewise; hay 85 kopny.

## One holder, a hamlet of his own

Sofon Timofeyevich held not a share in a multi-household settlement but a whole hamlet with its own name. In Sofonova the scribe noted one landholder's household and named neither peasant households nor co-holders. Recorded under Sofon:

- 4 chetverti of ploughed land of good soil;
- 36 chetverti of wild field in each field of the three-field system;
- 85 kopny of hay.

The ratio of ploughland to wild field shows an early stage in the development of the grant. The absence of peasant households does not necessarily mean they had recently been abandoned: unlike in the entries for Belyay and Ermak, the scribe did not write `двор пуст` ("household empty") here.

## Why the hamlet was called Sofonova

The name **Софонова** (Sofonova) matches the holder's name. For 16th-century pomestye geography this is a strong sign that the settlement was associated with Sofon — as its first holder, its founder or the person whose name became attached to the grant. But the book does not give the moment of founding and does not say outright "named after Sofon". So this is a well-grounded interpretation of the place name, not a literal statement of the source.

The present-day location of the hamlet has not been established. The only known historical reference point is the Cheremoshensky grove in Korchakov stan. No exact point can be placed on a modern map without a separate land-survey or cartographic source.

## Possibly three generations

In Bazdyrevo the same book records **Timofey, son of Mikula, Anfilogov**. Sofon is a Timofeyevich, bears the rare surname and lives in the same stan. A chain is therefore possible:

`Микула → Тимофей Микулович → Софон Тимофеевич`.

That would give three generations of one line. But the patronymic `Тимофеевич` does not by itself show that the father was the Timofey of Bazdyrevo: a man of the same name could have been someone else. The link remains a hypothesis until a direct statement of kinship or of land succession is found.

## Who compiled the book

The survey was carried out by the scribe **Dementy Yakovlev** and the clerk **Leonty Sofonov**. The coincidence of the clerk's surname with the hamlet's name is a chance resemblance: the document establishes no relationship between him and the holder.

The scribes checked the composition of pomestye grants and lands. They did not inspect Sofon's fitness for service and did not draw up a campaign roster.

## Service, novik status and campaigns

Sofon was a landholder of the Oryol service corporation. His particular category — regimental, stanitsa, samopal or siege — is not named in his entry. He is not marked as a novik or a nedorosl; age, length of service, horse and weapons are absent.

The document says nothing of an army assembly and names no campaign direction. Sofon's participation in a frontier patrol, a regimental campaign or siege service cannot be asserted merely from his holding of a pomestye.

## What it proves

- the existence of Sofon Timofeyevich Anfilogov;
- his sole landholder's household in the hamlet of Sofonova;
- the composition of his land and hay meadow;
- the historical existence of the hamlet below the Cheremoshensky grove.

## What it does not prove

- that Sofon personally founded the hamlet;
- that his father was Timofey Mikulovich of Bazdyrevo;
- the exact position of the hamlet on a modern map;
- any particular service, weapons or campaign.
