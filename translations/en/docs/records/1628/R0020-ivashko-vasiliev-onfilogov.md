# R0020 — Ivashko Vasilyev syn Onfilogov, 1627/28

## Original spelling in the publication

> Городовые. […] По 150 чети. […] Ивашко Васильев сын Онфилогов.

> *Translation:* Town servicemen. […] At 150 chetverti each. […] Ivashko, son of Vasily, Onfilogov.

The ellipses mark other names within the same sections of the list: the category heading is on fol. 119, the oklad heading on fol. 125.

## Extracted facts

- documentary form of the name: Ivashko Vasilyev syn Onfilogov;
- town of registration: Oryol;
- service category: **gorodovoy** (town serviceman);
- pomestye oklad: **150 chetverti**;
- archival location: fol. 125.

## What the oklad meant

150 chetverti is the reckoned norm of pomestye provision by which a man was placed in a service grade. It is not the area of land reliably in Ivan's actual possession, nor a cash payment. Without pomestye books it is impossible to establish where exactly his pomestye lay and how much land was actually settled or ploughed.

## Service context

The document is called the «подлинный» ("original") name list of the dvoryane, deti boyarskie and Cossacks of the year 7136. The Oryol part divides the men into vybornye (select), dvorovye (court), gorodovye (town) and noviki, and within the categories by pomestye oklad. Ivashko is among the gorodovye, that is, men of ordinary town service, and not in the separate section of noviki.

This is not a new muster and not a list of an army already sent on campaign. The compiler's name, a specific regiment, a campaign point and the place of the next assembly are not named in the available published fragment. A year earlier Ivan is named twice in the muster roll at Mtsensk; that document confirms the place of the muster, but does not allow the same official or campaign assignment to be carried over automatically to the list of 1627/28.

Oryol had been devastated in the Time of Troubles and was rebuilt only in 1636, so in 1627/28 "Oryol" designated the former service and district corporation of the Oryol deti boyarskie, not a fortress that had functioned continuously since its devastation.

## The biography continues

The full name and town match the Ivashko on fols. 105 and 149 of the 1626 muster roll and Ivashko Vasilyev syn Anfilogov on fol. 388 of the estimate book (smetnaya kniga) of 1641/42. The combination of name, patronymic and town makes the identification very likely, but each mention remains an independent event.

## What it does not prove

- Ivan's participation in any particular campaign;
- actual possession of all 150 chetverti;
- that he had a horse and particular weapons;
- novik status;
- kinship with Aleksei Vasilyevich on the basis of the same patronymic alone.
