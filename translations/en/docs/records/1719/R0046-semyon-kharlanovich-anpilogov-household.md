# R0046 — Semyon Kharlanovich Anpilogov (or Ampilogov) and his household, 20 June 1719

## Full transcription

> 1719 году июня в 20 день по указу великого государя царя и великого князя Петра Алексеевича всея Великия и Малыя и Белыя России самодержца, Белогородской провинции перед господином ландратом Никитою Борисовичем Вепрейским Хотмышского уезду деревни Задней однодворец рейтор Семен Хорланов сын Анпилогов [или Ампилогов] сказал по евангельской заповеди Господни вправду: живу я в помянутой деревне одним двором. От роду мне семьдесят лет. У меня два сына: Федор тридцати, Филип девяти лет, да приемыш Степан Антонов сын Слободин десяти лет. Итого мужеска пола четыре человека. А окроме тех помянутых душ мужеска пола в том моем дворе детей и братьев и родственников и крестьян и бобылей и задворных и деловых людей и их детей и свойственников и никаких пришлых и гулящих людей и работников утаенных у меня нет. А буде я сказал ложно или хотя едину душу мужеска пола утаил, и за то указал бы великий государь учинить мне наказание: бить кнутом нещадно, да сверх того за всякую душу утаенную из моего двора взять лучшего человека в солдаты не в зачет положенных рекрут. К сей сказке хотмышенин Лукьян Перов вместо Семена Анпилогова, что он сказал и в сей своей сказке написал самую истину, не утая ни единой мужеска пола души, по его прошению руку приложил.

> *Translation:* In the year 1719, on the 20th day of June, by decree of the great sovereign, Tsar and Grand Prince Pyotr Alekseyevich, Autocrat of all Great, Little and White Russia, in Belgorod province, before Mr Landrat Nikita Borisovich Vepreysky, the odnodvorets and reiter Semyon, son of Khorlan, Anpilogov [or Ampilogov], of the hamlet of Zadnyaya in Khotmyzhsk district, declared truthfully according to the Lord's commandment in the Gospel: I live in the said hamlet in one household. I am seventy years of age. I have two sons: Fyodor, thirty, Filip, nine years old, and a foster son, Stepan, son of Anton, Slobodin, ten years old. In all, four persons of the male sex. And apart from those said male souls, I have in that household of mine no concealed children, brothers, relatives, peasants, bobyli, zadvornye or delovye people (household and working dependants) or their children and in-laws, nor any newcomers, vagrants or labourers. And if I have spoken falsely or concealed even one male soul, then may the great sovereign order me punished for it: to be flogged mercilessly with the knout, and in addition, for every concealed soul, the best man of my household to be taken as a soldier, not counted against the required recruits. To this declaration, in place of Semyon Anpilogov, the Khotmyzhsk man Lukyan Perov, at his request, has set his hand, that he has stated and written in this declaration of his the very truth, concealing not a single male soul.

The transcription is normalised for reading: titlos (abbreviation marks) are expanded and punctuation added. The disputed superscript letter in the surname is kept as two possible readings.

| Filipp Semyonovich | son | 9 | about 1710 |
| Stepan Antonovich Slobodin | foster son | 10 | about 1709 |

In all, the declaration counts **four male souls**. Women were not listed in this document, so the absence of a wife or daughters in the table says nothing about whether they actually existed.

## The surname: Anpilogov or Ampilogov

After the initial `а` the consonant is written above the line before `пилоговъ`. On the available image it cannot be reliably distinguished as `н` or `м`. The transcriber of the page read `Анпилогов`, but palaeographically `Ампилогов` is also possible.

This is one of the most important early cases for the history of the surname: the variant `Ампилогов` cannot here be regarded as a late modernisation, although a final choice between the two readings is not yet possible.

## Who received the declaration

The skazka (declaration) was made before **Nikita Borisovich Vepreysky**, landrat of Belgorod province. A landrat was a provincial official of the Petrine era, responsible among other things for collecting and checking information for the revision (census).

Semyon himself was illiterate. At his request the document was signed by **Lukyan Perov** of Khotmyzhsk, who separately confirmed that Semyon had declared the truth and had not concealed a single male soul. Perov took part in drawing up the document; he was not a relative of the family.

## What «сказал по евангельской заповеди» means

The phrase «сказал по евангельской заповеди» ("declared according to the commandment in the Gospel") means the information was given under a religious oath and under threat of secular punishment. The form lists in detail whom it was forbidden to conceal: children, brothers, relatives, peasants, bobyli, labourers, newcomers and vagrants. Lying was punishable by merciless flogging with the knout and by giving up the best man of the household as a soldier over and above the usual recruit quota.

This shows how keen the state was on a full count of the male population: the revision became the basis of the poll tax and of recruit levies.

## Semyon's service position

Semyon is called both an **odnodvorets** (single-household freeholder) and a **reiter**.

- `рейтар` (reiter) indicates membership of the cavalry of the new-model (foreign-style) regiments;
- `однодворец` (odnodvorets) reflects the new estate status given in the Petrine era to the descendants of the small southern service population;
- his age of seventy makes him a member of the generation born around 1649 — still in the period when the Belgorod Line was being actively organised.

However, the 1719 entry does not check whether the seventy-year-old Semyon was actually still riding in the ranks. It has no horse, weapons, regiment, term of service or assessment of fitness.

## Was this a review, and where were troops assembling

No. This is the first Petrine revision, not a review of a reiter regiment. Vepreysky was taking information on households and souls, not holding a parade inspection here. No troops were assembling anywhere, and the declaration gives no campaign assignment for Semyon.

The threat to take a man «в солдаты» ("as a soldier") is a punishment for concealing a soul, not evidence that Fyodor, Filipp or Stepan had already been assigned to a regiment.

## Family details

- Semyon's patronymic is **Kharlanovich**, from the name Kharlan/Kharlampy. It is the first such patronymic found in the project and an important key to the Khotmyzhsk books of the 1640s.
- There are 21 years between Fyodor and Filipp. Such a large gap allows for Semyon's having married more than once, but the source does not name the mothers, so this is only a working hypothesis for research.
- Filipp was born when Semyon was about 61. The arithmetic follows from the declared ages and may have the error usual for revisions.
- Stepan is called a **foster son** but keeps his own patronymic and surname — Antonov syn Slobodin ("son of Anton, Slobodin"). He cannot automatically be included in the blood line of the Anpilogovs.

## Historical context

By 1719 the old southern service society was undergoing a deep restructuring. The descendants of deti boyarskie, Cossacks and men of regimental service were increasingly grouped into the category of odnodvortsy, and the state was moving from counting households to registering male souls. One folio brings two eras together: the old reiter Semyon and the new revision machinery of Peter I.

The hamlet of Zadnyaya lay in Khotmyzhsk district of Belgorod province. This is the first record found that links the Khotmyzhsk branch with a specific full name and a whole household, rather than just a folio number in an archival index.

## What it proves

- Semyon's full name, patronymic, age, place of residence and service status;
- Semyon's direct fatherhood of Fyodor and Filipp;
- that the four males lived together in one household;
- Stepan Slobodin's position as a foster son, not a named blood son;
- Semyon's illiteracy and Lukyan Perov's signature in his place;
- the existence of an early disputed reading `Анпилогов/Ампилогов`.

## What it does not prove

- the names of his wife, of the sons' mothers or of any daughters;
- that Semyon married more than once;
- a blood relationship between Stepan Slobodin and the head of household;
- Semyon's actual fitness for service in the ranks in 1719;
- the family's participation in any particular campaign;
- a final choice between the readings `Анпилогов` and `Ампилогов`.

## Folio numbering and the next search

The modern archival numbering and the Arabic numeral at the bottom give fol. 332; the old Cyrillic mark at the top corresponds to 331. So both references should be kept when ordering the file.

The main next search is for the name **Kharlan/Kharlampy Anpilogov** in the Khotmyzhsk books of the 1640s. By age he could turn out to be Semyon's father, but for now this is a working hypothesis, not an established link.
