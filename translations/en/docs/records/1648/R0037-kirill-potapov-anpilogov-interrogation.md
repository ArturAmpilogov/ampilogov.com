# R0037 — Interrogation of Kiryushka Potapov syn Anpilogov, 8 August 1648

## Original spelling

> Богородитцкого ж монастыря крестьянин Кирюшка Потапов сын Анпилогов пытан: и с пытки винился...

> *Translation:* Kiryushka, son of Potap, Anpilogov, a peasant of the same Bogoroditsky Monastery, was tortured, and under torture he confessed…

## Full transcription

Богородитцкого ж монастыря крестьянин Кирюшка Потапов сын Анпилогов пытан: и с пытки винился: перед съезжею избою кричал и Костентина убить прошал и двери выбивал и в ызбу кинулся он третей человек. А весь де завод учинился от протопопа, да от игуменьи, да от Кузьки Воденицына. И тот де Кузька им говорил, как шли к съезжей избе: «Подем де убьем Костянтина Теглева. Я де вам и починщик. На Москве де и не таких побивают». Да приходил де к ним протопопов сын, поп Иван, и говорил им мирским людем у приказной избы: отец де ево протопоп Григорей приказал убить Костянтина Теглева. А животов де у нево Костянтиновых дома лоскут кафтанной да лисица. И Костянтина де он убил. И как пошли они было из съезжой избы, и рукою де махал им поп Иван, и они де назад поворотились. А убили де они Костянтина с Макарком да с Костькою и с мирскими людми.

*Translation:* Kiryushka, son of Potap, Anpilogov, a peasant of the same Bogoroditsky Monastery, was tortured, and under torture he confessed: he had shouted in front of the administrative office and demanded that Konstantin be killed, and had broken down the doors, and he was the third man to rush into the building. And the whole thing, he said, was started by the archpriest, and the abbess, and Kuzka Vodenitsyn. And that Kuzka, he said, told them as they went to the administrative office: "Let us go and kill Konstantin Teglev. I will be your leader. In Moscow they kill bigger men than him." And the archpriest's son, the priest Ivan, came to them, he said, and told them, the townspeople, at the administrative office that his father the archpriest Grigory had ordered Konstantin Teglev to be killed. And of Konstantin's goods, he said, he has at home a piece of kaftan cloth and a fox fur. And he killed Konstantin. And when they were about to leave the administrative office, the priest Ivan waved his hand at them, he said, and they turned back. And they killed Konstantin, he said, with Makarko and with Kostka and with the townspeople.

## Extracted facts

- this is the fullest name formula in the file: `Кирюшка Потапов сын Анпилогов`;
- Kirill is directly called a peasant of the Kursk Bogoroditsky (Mother of God) Monastery;
- in testimony obtained under torture he admitted that he had shouted at the administrative office, demanded Teglev's killing, taken part in breaking down the doors and been one of the first to burst inside;
- by his own account, he had at home a length of kaftan cloth and a fox fur from Teglev's property;
- he laid the start of the uprising on the archpriest, the abbess and Kuzma Vodenitsyn, and the immediate signal on the priest Ivan.

## Who conducted the interrogation

The interrogation was part of the inquiry of the stolnik **Vasily Vasilyevich Buturlin** on 8 August 1648. The formula «пытан, и с пытки винился» ("was tortured, and under torture confessed") states directly that the confession was obtained by torture. So the card reliably establishes Kirill's name, patronymic and status and the fact that he was brought into the case, but the content of his self-incrimination cannot be accepted unconditionally.

## Why the uprising broke out

The streltsy commander **Konstantin Teglev** was involved in returning people from the privileged monastery slobody (settlements) and in transferring some monastery peasants to streltsy service. On 5 July a sovereign's charter brought from Moscow was read in the Kursk administrative office; it forbade him to continue such actions and handed the matter to the voevoda (governor) Fyodor Lodyzhensky. The dispute over the charter, the long-standing rivalry between the monasteries and the posad (trading quarter) and news of the Moscow riots led to the attack on the office and the killing of Teglev.

## Novik, serviceman or peasant?

Kirill is **not a novik** (newly enrolled serviceman). The document directly calls him a monastery peasant. He took part in an urban uprising, not in the sovereign's military service; no troops were being assembled, and he has no campaign direction or regimental affiliation.

## What it proves

- Kirill's full name and patronymic;
- his dependence on the Bogoroditsky Monastery;
- that he was brought into the investigation and made a confession under torture;
- the link between the forms `Анпилогов` and `Онпилогов` within one investigation file.

## What it does not prove

- that the confession was voluntary;
- a genealogical link with other bearers of the surname;
- that every person Kirill named actually gave orders.

## Reading notes

The normalised name `Кирилл Потапович Анпилогов` (Kirill Potapovich Anpilogov) is derived directly from the formula `Кирюшка Потапов сын Анпилогов`, not reconstructed from a match with another document.
