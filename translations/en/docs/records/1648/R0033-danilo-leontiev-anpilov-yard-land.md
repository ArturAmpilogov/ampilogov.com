# R0033 — Regimental Cossack Danilo Anpilov: household plot and ploughland, 1647/48

## Original name

> Данило Левонтьев сын Анпилов.

> *Translation:* Danilo, son of Levonty, Anpilov.

## The full documentary formula, according to the publication

Danilo is a **regimental Cossack transferred from Oskol**. In Tsaryov-Alekseyev he
was measured out:

- a household plot **20 sazhens** long and **12 sazhens** wide;
- ploughland of **10 chetverti in a field, and in the other two likewise**.

The household plot covered 240 square sazhens. The land formula refers to the three-field system:
10 chetverti fell to each of the three fields, not to the whole allotment taken together.
It describes the standard allotment; the entry does not confirm that the whole area was actually
ploughed.

## Who made the allotment

The land and household plots for the transferees from various towns were measured out by **Yelizary Ivanovich
Yakovlev** of Kaluga, by the sovereign's decree and on the orders of the voevoda (governor) Prince **Vasily Petrovich
Lvov**. Deti boyarskie and stanichniki (scouts) were entitled to 12 chetverti in each
field, and Cossacks, to whom Danilo belonged, to 10.

Thus Lvov was responsible for organising the new town, while the actual
surveyor and compiler of the allotment was Yakovlev. This is not a military review.

## Where Danilo was moved from, and why

«Сведён с Оскола» ("transferred from Oskol") means an official transfer from the old town of Oskol, later
called Stary Oskol. Danilo was not sent on a short campaign: he was included in the
permanent garrison and given an economic basis for his family's life.

Tsaryov-Alekseyev was built as a fortress of the Belgorod Line. It blocked the area of
the Kalmius sakma (steppe track) and other routes of Tatar raids. A regimental Cossack was to
perform frontier service from the new town; this entry records no separate sortie or
specific regiment of a field army.

## A novik or an experienced serviceman

Danilo is directly called a regimental Cossack from Oskol and a resettler. There is no formula of first
enrolment, so he cannot be considered a novik (newly enrolled serviceman). The earlier list of those chosen
for permanent residence already calls him the head of a family with two growing sons.

## What it does not prove

- that the whole allotted land was actually ploughed;
- that he had particular weapons and a horse;
- Danilo's participation in any particular battle;
- that the resettlement was voluntary.
