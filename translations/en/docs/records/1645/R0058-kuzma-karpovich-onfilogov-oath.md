# R0058 — Kuzma Karpovich Onfilogov swears allegiance to the new tsar, 1645

## The line on the folio

> Куз[ь]ма Карповъ сын Онфилогов.

> *Translation:* Kuzma, son of Karp, Onfilogov.

The entry is on folio 1155 of the krestoprivodnaya kniga (oath book). The given name `Кузьма`, the patronymic from Karp and the surname can be read on a line of their own; there is nothing next to it about an oklad or weapons.

## One man, two spellings in three years

In the smetnaya kniga (estimate book) of 1641/42 the same man is recorded as **Кузма Карпов сын Анфилогов** (Kuzma, son of Karp, Anfilogov). In 1645 he became **Онфилогов** (Onfilogov). The rare combination of given name and patronymic, Oryol district and the close dates all match, so both entries are merged into one profile.

This is the second strong example of the beginning of the surname changing for a specific person. For Ivan and Mark in the Mtsensk file of 1626 the form changed between roll calls of the same summer; for Kuzma the shift `Анфилогов → Онфилогов` is visible between two different kinds of service records.

## Karp's possible family

An unverified research extract of 1625 names Martyn and Kuzma as sons of an Oryol Karp. In 1626–1628 a Mark Karpovich is in service, and in 1641/42 and 1645 a Kuzma Karpovich. Karp Mikulovich Anfilogov is known as early as 1594/95.

These data suggest a working scheme:

`Карп Микулович → Мартын, Кузьма и, возможно, Марк Карповичи` (Karp Mikulovich → Martyn, Kuzma and possibly Mark Karpovich).

But it is not considered established. The 1625 extract does not yet have an exact shelfmark or image; Mark is not named in it, and the name Karp could have belonged to another man. Only Kuzma's patronymic is reliably proven: his father was called Karp.

## The historical context of the oath

After the death of Mikhail Fyodorovich on 13 July 1645, servicemen were brought to kiss the cross to Aleksey Mikhaylovich. The Oryol part belongs to a large block of seven towns and bears the skrepa (certifying signature) of the clerk **Mikhail Stepanov**.

The book attests Kuzma's presence and his oath. The names of the priest and the voevoda (governor) are not given on this folio. The document is not a muster desyatnya (service register): it does not inspect horse, pishchal (harquebus), sabre or armour and does not assign an oklad.

## Novik status and campaigns

In 1645 Kuzma is not called a novik (newly enrolled serviceman). His service is already confirmed by the estimate book of 1641/42, so this is a man of earlier service, not a young landholder being enrolled for the first time.

The entry contains no campaign assignment. The oath was a nationwide act after the tsar's accession, not the assembly of a particular regiment. Kuzma cannot be credited with a posting to the southern frontier or to a specific campaign without another document.

## What it proves

- Kuzma Karpovich's oath in 1645;
- the surname form `Онфилогов`;
- the shift of form `Анфилогов → Онфилогов` for a man with the same full name;
- the continuation of his documented service after 1641/42.

## What it does not prove

- that his father was precisely the Karp Mikulovich of 1594/95;
- that he was a brother of Martyn or Mark Karpovich;
- particular weapons, oklad or campaign;
- the exact date of the oath.
