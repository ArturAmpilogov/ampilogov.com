# R0059 — Anton Ivanovich Onfilogov in the Oryol oath, 1645

## The line on the folio

> Онтон Иванов сын Онфилогов.

> *Translation:* Onton (Anton), son of Ivan, Onfilogov.

The given name is written with an initial `О`: **Онтон** (Onton). In modern form it is rendered as Anton, but the literal reading is kept. The surname also begins with `Он-` — `Онфилогов`.

## A possible son of Ivan of Belaya

A research extract of 1625 reports that Ivan Anfilogov in the hamlet of Belaya had sons Ilya, Vasily and Anton. Twenty years later an Anton Ivanovich Onfilogov appears in the Oryol oath.

The given name, patronymic, rare surname stem and district all match. The age sequence is also possible: a son named in 1625 could take the oath on his own in 1645. But the 1625 extract does not yet have an exact archival shelfmark or image, and the oath does not name the hamlet of Belaya. So the link is strong, but not yet direct.

If it is confirmed, Anton will join a short line:

`Иван Анфилогов → Илья, Василий и Антон Ивановичи` (Ivan Anfilogov → Ilya, Vasily and Anton Ivanovich).

Ivan himself is sometimes identified with Ivan Pershin Anfilogov, who held a share in Belaya in 1594/95. That would add one more generation, but each of the two links requires intermediate pomestye books.

## Who drew up the oath

The krestoprivodnye knigi (oath books) of seven towns are in a file bearing the skrepa (certifying signature) of the clerk **Mikhail Stepanov**. Anton's folio belongs to the Oryol part. It gives neither the name of the priest who received the oath nor the name of the local voevoda (governor); the source attests the result of the ceremony rather than describing its course in detail.

The occasion is reconstructed from the date and the type of document: after the death of Mikhail Fyodorovich on 13 July 1645, servicemen swore allegiance to his son Aleksey Mikhaylovich.

## Service status, novik status and campaigns

Anton is recorded among the Oryol servicemen and personally included in the list of those who took the oath. The line gives no narrower rank or type of service. He is not called a novik (newly enrolled serviceman), a nedorosl (son not yet of service age) or retired.

The book has no information on a pomestye, oklad, horse or weapons. Nor is there a campaign direction: kissing the cross confirmed loyalty to the new tsar but did not in itself mean sending out troops. Desyatni (service registers) and muster lists are needed for a military profile of Anton.

## What it proves

- the presence of Anton Ivanovich in the Oryol part of the oath;
- the literal forms `Онтон` and `Онфилогов`;
- that he took the oath to the new sovereign;
- the continuation of the surname form in Oryol district up to 1645.

## What it does not prove

- that Anton was a son of precisely Ivan of Belaya;
- that the Ivan of 1625 is identical with the Ivan Pershin of 1594/95;
- Anton's particular rank, weapons or pomestye;
- participation in a military campaign.
