# R0057 — Aleksey Vasilyevich Onfilogov swears allegiance to Aleksey Mikhaylovich, 1645

## The line on the folio

> Алексей Васил[ь]ев сын Онфилогов.

> *Translation:* Aleksey, son of Vasily, Onfilogov.

The name stands on a separate line on the verso of folio 1153. The photograph makes it possible to check not only the surname but also the position of the entry within the continuous Oryol list.

## Why the book was compiled

On 13 July 1645 Tsar Mikhail Fyodorovich died. His sixteen-year-old son Aleksey Mikhaylovich came to the throne, and the inhabitants of the service towns were brought to krestnoye tselovaniye — kissing the cross, the oath of allegiance to the new sovereign. The names of those who took the oath were sent to the Razryad Prikaz (Military Service Chancellery) in special krestoprivodnye knigi (oath books).

The file covers Oryol, Kromy, Kursk, Bely, Chuguyev, Khotmyzhsk and Volny. Folios 1153–1183 lie at the start of this block and belong to Oryol. The whole section bears the skrepa (certifying signature across the folios) of the clerk **Mikhail Stepanov**. This means he was officially responsible for the integrity of the book, but does not prove that he wrote every line in his own hand.

The folio itself names neither the priest before whom the cross was kissed nor the Oryol voevoda (governor) who organised the ceremony. Their names cannot be filled in from a general list of town officials without the heading folio.

## A probable continuation of earlier service

Oryol documents already know an Aleksey Vasilyevich with the same rare surname stem:

- in the review list of 1620/21 — **Олексей Васильев Анфилохов** (Oleksey Vasilyev Anfilokhov);
- at two Mtsensk roll calls of 1626 — **Олешка Васильев сын Онфилогов** (Oleshka, son of Vasily, Onfilogov);
- in the list of 1627/28 — a novik (newly enrolled serviceman) enrolled in 1621/22 with a pomestye oklad (land entitlement) of 200 chetverti;
- in the name extract from the smetnaya kniga (estimate book) of 1641/42 — **Алексей Васильев Анфилогов** (Aleksey Vasilyev Anfilogov);
- in the oath of 1645 — **Aleksey Vasilyevich Onfilogov**.

The rare full name, a single service town, an unbroken sequence of entries and a gap of only about three years between the last two documents allow them to be merged into one profile. This is a high-confidence identification, although the scribes themselves gave no direct cross-reference to the earlier entry.

By 1645 Aleksey had served for about a quarter of a century and was no longer a novik. He is first called a novik in connection with the enrolment of 1621/22; the oath book of 1645 gives no such status.

## What the oath entry means

Aleksey was present among the Oryol servicemen and took the obligatory oath to the new tsar. This records his personal presence for political and service purposes, but it is not a military review. The book does not give:

- a land or money oklad;
- horse, weapons and armour;
- family members;
- a regiment or place of future service;
- the direction of a campaign.

This entry did not send the army anywhere. The point of the event was the change of sovereign and the confirmation of loyalty, not preparation for a particular campaign.

## What it proves

- the presence of Aleksey Vasilyevich Onfilogov in the Oryol part of the oath;
- the surname form `Онфилогов` in 1645;
- that he took the oath to the new sovereign;
- that the entry is part of the book certified by the clerk Mikhail Stepanov.

## What it does not prove

- a direct scribal cross-reference to Aleksey's earlier entries;
- the exact day and place of the ceremony within Oryol;
- a particular service category, weapons or campaign;
- kinship with the Vasilyevichi named in the unverified extract of 1625.
