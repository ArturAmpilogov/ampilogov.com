# R0047 — The Village of Onfilogovo in Tver District, 1539/1540

## Original fragment

> За Стефанидою за Ивановою женою Юрлова с. Николское Свѣчино, а въ немъ церковь Никола чюдотворецъ: дв. боярской, во дв. попъ, а людей еѣ 2 дв., дв. пустъ; пашни въ полѣ 64 четьи, сѣна 100 коп. Къ тому жъ селу деревни: дер. Онфилогово: 4 дв.; пашни въ полѣ 30 четьи, сѣна 30 коп.

> *Translation:* Held by Stefanida, wife of Ivan Yurlov: the village of Nikolskoye Svechino, and in it the church of Nicholas the Wonderworker; a manor household, in a household the priest, and her people's households 2, one household empty; ploughland 64 chetverti in a field, hay 100 kopny. Belonging to the same village, hamlets: the hamlet of Onfilogovo: 4 households; ploughland 30 chetverti in a field, hay 30 kopny.

## What was found here

The cadastral book (pistsovaya kniga) names the **hamlet of Onfilogovo** — four households, 30 chetverti of ploughland in a field and a hay meadow of 30 kopny (haycocks). It belonged to the complex of the village of Nikolskoye Svechino in Khorvach volost of Goretsky stan, Tver district.

This is not the surname of any particular inhabitant recorded in the book. A name ending in `-ово` is formed from the personal name **Onfilog**, a colloquial form of the name Amphilochius (Amfilokhy). The eponym who gave the place its name must have lived before the survey, but the book gives neither his full name nor the date the hamlet was founded.

## Who compiled the survey

The cadastral book of the year 7048 was compiled by the sovereign's scribes:

- **Ivan Petrovich Zabolotsky**;
- **Mikhail Ivanov Usov-Tatishchev**.

The folios were countersigned by the clerk (dyak) **Semeyka Karpov**, and from fol. 179 by the clerk **Andrey Myasnov**. They described the court, pomestye and black (crown peasant) lands of Tver district, counting households, ploughland, hay and abandoned plots.

## Stefanida Yurlova's holding

At the time of the survey the village with its hamlets was held by **Stefanida, wife of Ivan Yurlov** — the widow of Ivan Yurlov. The formula `Иванова жена` ("Ivan's wife") points to her husband and is not Stefanida's patronymic.

In all she held a village and six hamlets: Onfilogovo, Sadykovo, Ishkovo, Ostrogino, Voronino and Popovo. The summary of the entry gives 18 households, 19 people, one empty household, 206 chetverti of ploughland in one field and 260 kopny of hay.

## What became of the holding later

The inspection book (dozornaya kniga) of 1551–1554 again records under Stefanida Yurlova **the village of Nikolskoye Svechino with its hamlets**. The whole holding was assessed at a third of a sokha. The scribe then added: **«И тому селу и деревням Степанида писма не давала»** ("And for that village and hamlets Stepanida gave no deed").

This means that at the inspection she did not present the title deeds. The entry does not prove that the deeds never existed: it is precisely an earlier document that may have preserved the name of a previous owner, of the founder, or of the person with whom the name Onfilogovo was connected.

In the 1877 edition this line is at the bottom of **p. 230**, not p. 231. Both books survive in a single volume: **RGADA, f. 1209, op. 1, book 466**. The cadastral book occupies fols. 1–327v, the inspection book fols. 328–773v; the surviving manuscript is a copy made in 1576–1578.

There is also an administrative discrepancy: in the book of 1539/1540 the holding is placed in Goretsky stan, while the entry of 1551–1554 stands under the heading of Yuryevsky stan. The reason cannot yet be established without checking the manuscript or a new edition.

## Where it was

The earlier marker near Nikolo-Svechino in Yaroslavl Oblast turned out to be a false match of names. On the scholarly map of 16th-century Tver district, Nikolskoye (Svechino) is placed in Khorvach volost, west of Pervitino and near Romanovo, in the area of the present-day boundary between the Staritsa and Zubtsov districts.

The source layer of the scholarly GIS gives for object No. 2188, «Николское Свечино», the point **56.322586, 35.204732**. The object's attributes list Khorvach volost of Yuryevsky stan, the category of pomestye lands and the holder «Стефанида Иванова жена Юрлова» ("Stefanida, wife of Ivan Yurlov"); the 1551–1554 layer repeats the same point. The earlier reference point near the site of Nikolskoye-Dorofeyevo has therefore been dropped. The new coordinate is a scholarly reconstruction of the centre of the holding, but still not a located point for the dependent hamlet of Onfilogovo itself.

## Why this matters for the surname

Onfilogovo shows that the name `Онфилог` (Onfilog) was alive and capable of producing place names no later than the first third of the 16th century. The chain of derivation looks like this:

`Амфилохий → Онфилог / Анфилог / Анпилог → Онфилогово или Анпилогов`.

The Tver find confirms the etymology of the surname, but not that the Oryol family came from Tver. There is no documented kinship between the hamlet and Oryol Anfilogovs.

## Was this a military muster

No. This is a description of land and economy. It contains no review of servicemen, noviki (newly enrolled servicemen), weapons, horses or campaign assignments. The hamlet was not "mustered" anywhere; the scribes recorded its households and lands.

## What it proves

- the existence of the name `Онфилогово` in 1539/1540;
- the presence of four households in the hamlet;
- the use of the stem `Онфилог-` in 16th-century Russian toponymy;
- that the hamlet belonged to Stefanida Yurlova's holding.
- that Stefanida Yurlova still held it in 1551–1554;
- that she did not present deeds for the village and hamlets at the inspection.

## What it does not prove

- that the inhabitants of this hamlet bore the surname Onfilogov;
- the name, status or lifetime of the eponym;
- kinship between the Tver inhabitants and Oryol Anfilogovs;
- military service or a move from Tver to Oryol.
- that no old deeds existed at all;
- the exact location of the hamlet of Onfilogovo within the holding.

## What to look for next

It would be useful to trace Onfilogovo in later cadastral books and on the plans of the General Land Survey. That would show whether the name survived and where exactly the hamlet lay, but would not in itself establish a genealogical link.
