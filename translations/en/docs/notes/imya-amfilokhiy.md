# Where the Surname Comes From: the Baptismal Name Amphilochius

The page for the surname «Анпилов» (Anpilov) on the «Наши Предки» (Our Ancestors) wiki gives a one-line etymology:

> имя Ампил — от крестильного имени Амфилохий — «кругом опасность» (греч.)

> *Translation:* the name Ampil — from the baptismal name Amphilochius — "danger all around" (Greek)

In itself this is a claim without a source reference, but it explains what
we have been observing in the documents all month.

## Why This Matters for Our Sample

All four forms that we meet in the files of the Razryad Prikaz (Military Service Chancellery) —
**Анфилогов, Онфилогов, Анпилогов, Онпилогов** (Anfilogov, Onfilogov, Anpilogov, Onpilogov) — go back to a single stem,
*Амфилох-* (Amphiloch-):

- **Анфилогов / Онфилогов** (Anfilogov / Onfilogov) — closest to the original «Амфилохий» (Amfilokhy): both the
  «ф» (f) and the «-лог-» (-log-) from «-лох-» (-lokh-) are kept.
- **Анпилогов / Онпилогов** (Anpilogov / Onpilogov) — the «ф» (f) has turned into «п» (p); the stem remains.
- **Анпилов / Ампилов** (Anpilov / Ampilov) — the «-ог-» (-og-) has dropped out and the stem is shortened.
- **Ампилогов** (Ampilogov) — keeps the «м» (m) of «Амфилохий»; this is the form by which the family
  is known today.

So the dispute "Anpilogov or Ampilogov" is not a dispute about two different surnames
but about different stages of one and the same name. The alternation of ф/п and н/м is commonplace
in 17th-century cursive (skoropis), where both letters are often superscript and are almost
indistinguishable in writing. That is exactly what we see on folio 332 of the Khotmyzhsk statement (skazka) of 1719:
the superscript letter there reads equally well as «н» (n) and as «м» (m).

## The Name within the Family

The name is not only at the root of the surname but within the family itself: the project has
**P0143 — Anfilokhy (Корчева / Korcheva, 1848)**. In other words, the baptismal name survived in the family
until the middle of the 19th century.

Feast days of saints named Amphilochius in the church calendar (mesyatseslov): 5 April (the martyr Amphilochius
of Illyria), 12 May (the Venerable Amphilochius of Pochaiv), 1 October
(the hieromartyr Amphilochius Skvortsov), 23 October (St Amphilochius,
Bishop of the Caves, of Vladimir-Volynsky), 25 October (the Venerable Amphilochius
of Glushitsa), 6 December (St Amphilochius, Bishop of Iconium).

The last is the most likely source of the name for 16th–17th-century Russia:
Amphilochius of Iconium was widely venerated, and his December feast day is close to
St Nicholas's Day, when baptisms were especially frequent.

## Direct Documentary Proof: the Name Anpilog in Oryol District Itself

Until now the etymology rested on a single line from a wiki without a source. Now
it rests on a cadastral book (pistsovaya kniga).

**«Писцовые книги Московского государства» (Cadastral Books of the Muscovite State), pt. I, sect. 2, p. 948** — Oryol
district, Korchakov stan, a village on the Gosudarnino selishche (deserted settlement site) on the river Orel below
the Korchakov forest:

> За Кондрашкомъ за Вавиловымъ сыномъ Онаньина, **что было за дѣдомъ его за
> Анпилогомъ (sic) за Ивановымъ сыномъ Онаньина** въ тойже дер. на Государнинѣ
> селищѣ, на р. на Орлѣ, подъ Корчаковымъ лѣсомъ, на его жеребью:
> дв. помѣщиковъ; пашни паханые добр. земли 7 четьи да дикого поля 44 чети
> въ полѣ, а въ дву потомужъ, сѣна 110 коп.

> *Translation:* Held by Kondrashko, son of Vavila, Onanyin, **which was held by his grandfather
> Anpilog (sic), son of Ivan, Onanyin**, in the same village on the Gosudarnino
> selishche, on the river Orel, below the Korchakov forest, on his share (zherebye):
> the landholder's household; ploughed land of good soil 7 chetverti and wild field 44 chetverti
> in one field, and in the other two likewise; hay 110 kopny (stacks).

Here **Anpilog is a personal name, not a surname**: the man's surname is Onanyin.
He is the grandfather of a serviceman recorded as an adult landholder in 1594/95.
The working genealogical estimate of his birth is **1500–1535**: two
generation intervals of 20–30 years separate the grandfather from the adult grandson. This is not
a date from the source but an explicitly labelled calculation. And he held land in the very same
Korchakov stan where, half a century later, Anfilogovs and Anpilogovs are recorded.

The chain "name → patronymic → surname" is closed within a single district and a single
stan:

| stage | document | form |
|---|---|---|
| personal name | ПКМГ I/2, p. 948, Korchakov stan | **Анпилог** Иванов сын Онаньин (**Anpilog**, son of Ivan, Onanyin) |
| surname from the father | ПКМГ I/2, pp. 975, 979, 1009, 1011, 1016 | Беляй Васильев сын **Онфилогова**, Осип Васильев сын **Анфилогова**, Иван Нечаев сын **Анпилогова** (Belyay, son of Vasily, **Onfilogov**; Osip, son of Vasily, **Anfilogov**; Ivan, son of Nechay, **Anpilogov**) |
| baptismal name in the family | Korcheva, 1848 (P0143) | **Анфилохий** (Anfilokhy) |

In the project one cadastral episode is split into two records: **R0048** is devoted to Anpilog Ivanovich himself as the former holder, **R0049** to his grandson Kondraty and the state of the share in 1594/95. Source cards: `PUB-PKOU-1594-P948-ANPILOG` and `PUB-PKOU-1594-P948-ANPILOG-ONANIN`.

A caveat: the Onanyins are not our family, and no kinship with them has been proved. The record
proves not the origin of the family but the origin of the **word**.

The Tver Onfilogovo of 1539/40 and the Oryol Anpilog Onanyin show
that this name cluster was alive in different parts of the country already in the first
half of the 16th century. But they **do not form a migration route**: there is no document
linking the Tver village with the Oryol people.

## Place Name: the Village of Onfilogovo, Tver District, 1539/40

In the same volume, on p. 111 — Tver district, Khorvach volost, Goretsky stan, held by
the widow Stefanida Yurlova: the village (selo) of Nikolskoye Svechino, with its hamlets, the first
of which is **Онфилогово** (Onfilogovo) — 4 households, 30 chetverti of ploughland, 30 kopny of hay. The edition's OCR
gave «Онфилозово» (Onfilozovo); **the reading has been checked against the page scan and confirmed**:
the name is set in italics and the «г» (g) is clearly visible.

A village named after a man called Onfilog means that such a man lived
there no later than the beginning of the 16th century. This has no kinship link with the Oryol families: the
significance of the find is that the name was alive and in everyday use throughout central Russia before
our surname was formed from it.

Record: `PUB-PKMG-1540-TVER-P111-ONFILOGOVO`, scan `public/archive/evidence/publications/piscovye-knigi-1539-1540-tver/pages/0111.png`.
