# Is It Worth Searching for the Family in the Novgorod Books?

Short answer: it is worth checking, but expectations are modest. Below is why and how.

## Why Look North at All

The earliest record in the whole sample is not a southern one. It is **«Тонкой Онфилогов» (Tonkoy Onfilogov),
1536/37, the small village of Priluk, Gorodskoy stan, Uglich district**
(S. M. Kashtanov, after the Trinity copy book; РНБ, Pogodin collection, No. 1846,
fol. 65v). The household is a non-ploughing one, which means a peasant rather than a
serviceman. That is more than a hundred years before the Belgorod Line and not the
south at all.

So a north-central layer of the family existed, and the question "are there any of
ours in the north" is a legitimate one, not an idle one.

## What the "Novgorod Records" Are in Fond 210

The Novgorod Desk of the Razryad Prikaz (Military Service Chancellery) was in charge
of the north-west: Novgorod, Pskov, Velikiye Luki, Toropets, Rzheva, Zavolochye. In
fond 210 this means:

- **op. 3** — Books of the Novgorod Desk;
- **op. 11** — Scrolls (stolbtsy) of the Novgorod Desk.

And the books have their own alphabetical index: **index No. 118, «Алфавиты изъ книгъ
Новгородскаго Стола, кн. 59—75» (Alphabets from the Books of the Novgorod Desk, books 59–75), 599 scans**. It is arranged the same way as
the Belgorod volumes 121–135: surname → «№ Общiй» ("general No.", the book number) → «№ Част.»
("part No.", the folio number). The method carries over one to one; nothing needs to be invented.

## What to Expect

Modest results. The Novgorod Desk covers the servicemen of the north-west, whereas the
Uglich record of 1536 is a peasant household in Zamoskovye (the lands around Moscow). A different region,
a different social estate — no direct connection should be expected. But the check is cheap: two letters
(А and О) in seventeen books — one or two passes using the same method as for the
Belgorod Desk.

It is worth remembering separately that Uglich and Yaroslavl belong not to the Novgorod
Desk but to the Vladimir and Moscow Desks. If the Uglich line is to be pursued seriously, one
should look at **op. 1 (Books of the Moscow Desk)** and **op. 2 (Books of the Vladimir
Desk)**, and their alphabetical indexes, not the Novgorod ones.

## Order of Work

1. Index No. 118, letters А and О across all seventeen books (59–75).
2. If anything turns up — the entries of inventory 3 for the corresponding files, leads by folio.
3. Regardless of the result — check whether alphabetical indexes to the books of the
   Moscow and Vladimir Desks have been put online: the Uglich line of 1536 is more
   logically searched there.

## A Trap When Searching Online

There is a historian with the same surname, **Grigory Nikolayevich Anpilogov** (1902–1987),
who published «Новгородские и псковские грамоты XV—XVI вв.» (Novgorod and Pskov Charters of the 15th–16th Centuries) and a Ryazan
cadastral reference book (pripravochnaya kniga). He has no connection with the family, but any query
like "Анпилогов Новгород" (Anpilogov Novgorod) returns him first of all. This is not a find; it is noise.
