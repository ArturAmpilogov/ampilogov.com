# Statements (Skazki) of 1675–1700: Eighteen Files the Project Did Not Have

The previous export from the database of 17th-century servicemen (`SLUZHILYE-XVII-V36`) was
deliberately cut off: its description of method states plainly — «отобраны формы
Анпилогов и Онпилогов **до 1649 года**» ("the forms Anpilogov and Onpilogov were selected **up to 1649**"). Everything later was not entered into the project.

Meanwhile, on its sheet of statements (skazki) the database gives **24 lines for 1675–1700: 18 files and 49
persons submitting statements**. Seventeen of the eighteen files are not mentioned anywhere in the project.

The machine-readable export is in
[`data/genealogy/indexes/skazki-1675-1700.json`](../../data/genealogy/indexes/skazki-1675-1700.json).

## What These Documents Are

Skazki are servicemen's own statements about their service, pomestye (service estate) and family,
collected town by town. For 1697 this is a mass review, for 1675 the statements of the
Belgorod Desk, for 1700 those of Kromy. Unlike desyatni (service registers) and muster
rolls, a skazka speaks in the first person and often names the father, the children and the previous
place of service. For our task this is the best type of document in the whole 17th century.

## What Matters Here

**The Kursk cluster of 1697.** File 210-12-1559 has thirteen persons submitting statements in
one file, in three spellings at once: Анпилогов, Анпилогав, Онпилогов. This is the
largest concentration of the surname in all 17th-century documents known to the project,
larger than the Oryol one of 1647/48. File 1491 with four
persons belongs here too — so for Kursk in 1697 the total comes to **seventeen people**.

**Oboyan gets a continuation.** Until now the Oboyan line broke off with the
desyatnya of 1651 and Sergey Grigoryevich. Now there is Oboyan 1675 (file 745) and
Oboyan 1697 (file 1628) — one person in each. Twenty-four and
forty-six years after Sergey. These two files are the cheapest to order:
a skazka names the father and children, and so may link up directly with Sidor and
Yeremey.

**Livny file 1482 is not fully accounted for.** It is already cited in the project as
`S-SHCHIGRY-1697` — the statement of Akim Anpilogov, in which Tit is called his grandson. But
the index shows **five persons with three spellings** in this file, whereas the project
has examined one story. The other four statements of the same file have not been read.

**Khotmyzhsk is confirmed twice.** File 1627 (1697) falls exactly on the find from the
handwritten alphabetical indexes of the Razryad Archive, where for Khotmyzhsk the surname is confidently
written «Анпилоговъ» (Anpilogov) in files 210-6д-4 and 210-6д-9 for 1639–1642. So
the Khotmyzhsk branch stays in place for almost sixty years.

**Three new towns.** Verkhososensk, Olshansk and Nedrygailov have never appeared in the
project before. Olshansk and Verkhososensk are towns of the Belgorod Line;
Nedrygailov is a Sloboda town, already beyond the earlier geography.

For Verkhososensk the role has now been clarified: the open surname list of file
210-12-1616 marks Anpilov as a **stepson** (pasynok), not an independent person submitting a statement.
This gives no name or folio, but it changes the search method. The bearer of the surname may
turn up inside his stepfather's statement after the mother's remarriage, so the request
should be made for the line «Анпилов / пасынок» ("Anpilov / stepson"), and in later revisions one should check not
only the heads of households but also widows, stepsons and notes within other surnames.
[LEAD-210-12-1616-VERKHOSOSENSK-1697]

**The "Anpilov or Anpilogov" dispute gets statistics.** For Belgorod and
Verkhososensk the database reads almost exclusively «Анпилов» (Anpilov), for Kursk and Khotmyzhsk —
«Анпилогов» (Anpilogov); for Olshansk and Livny both forms stand in the same file. This is exactly the
picture given by the alphabetical index of the Razryad Archive, and it confirms the conclusion of
`alfavity-belgorodskogo-stola.md`: the forms are divided geographically, not only
by the hand of the scribe.

**For Timoshevka, file 1622 changes the priority.** The open register does not merely assign
the single Anpilov to Belgorod but places him under the heading of reiters (reytary, cavalrymen of the new order). Under the same
heading are the Gostishchevs, Ozerovs, Selyukovs, Chernyaevs and Martynovs — almost
the entire family circle of the successive Timoshevka marriages of 1816. It is especially
important that the compiler describes the statements in the file as containing the size and
**the settlement of the pomestye**. So a single Anpilov folio can test the whole
micro-region of Sazhnoye—Nepkhayevo—Ozerovo—Gostishchevo in one reading. For now the shared heading
means a common service circle, not one family or one household.

## Order of Ordering

1. **210-12-1622** — Belgorod 1697. For the question of the origin of Timoshevka: find
   the folio of Anpilov the reiter and read the name of his pomestye.
2. **210-12-745** and **210-12-1628** — Oboyan 1675 and 1697. Small files, a direct
   continuation of Sergey Grigoryevich's line.
3. **210-12-1559** — Kursk 1697. Thirteen statements; the most valuable file in the sample.
4. **210-12-1482** — Livny 1697, finish reading the other four statements.
5. **210-12-1491** — Kursk 1697.
6. **210-14-373** — Kromy 1700.
7. **210-12-1627** — Khotmyzhsk 1697, link with the alphabetical indexes of 1639–1642.

## Caveat

A database line gives only the form of the surname, the town, the call number and the number of persons. It has
no name, no patronymic and no folio, and the reading of the surname was made by the compiler of the database and
has not been checked against the image. None of these lines becomes a Record until
a folio or a full publication of the text is found.
