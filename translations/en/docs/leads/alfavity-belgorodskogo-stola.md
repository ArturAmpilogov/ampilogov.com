# Handwritten Alphabetical Indexes of the Razryad Archive: Thirteen Folio Numbers for Free

The "Dom Predkov" index gives the file call number, the year, the town and the name — and
stops there. The author supplies the folio number for a fee. It turned out that for
a whole layer of files this number can be obtained free of charge: in the 19th century
the State Razryad Archive compiled **handwritten alphabetical indexes** (alfavity) to its books,
and РГАДА has put them online page by page.

## What They Are and What They Cover

The printed inventory 6д says so plainly, on its very first page:

> Книги Белгородского стола обнимают период от 1626 г. по 1737 г. и состоят
> в хранении Архива в количестве 201 номера. К 182 книгам Белгородского
> стола имеется алфавит в 15 томах (№№ 121—135).

> *Translation:* The books of the Belgorod Desk cover the period from 1626 to 1737 and are
> kept in the Archive in a quantity of 201 numbers. For 182 books of the Belgorod
> Desk there is an alphabetical index in 15 volumes (Nos. 121–135).

So the indexes **210-ukaz_121 … 135** are the alphabetical indexes to inventory **6д**,
«Книги Белгородского стола» (Books of the Belgorod Desk). The numbering of the books in the indexes matches the
numbering of the files in the inventory: this has been checked against the years in the headings — book No. 1 is dated
7137, and entry 1 of the inventory is also 7137; book No. 9 — 7150, entry 9 — 7150;
book No. 18 — 7155–56, entry 18 — 7155–56. Seven matches out of seven.

The arrangement is simple: a letter, and within the letter, book after book, each with
the heading "No. so-and-so of such-and-such a year", beneath it a column of surnames and next to them
the folio numbers. Within a letter the order is not alphabetical but follows the reading of the book.
The later volumes (for example No. 130) are already made on printed forms with the columns
«Фамилии древних чинов | № Общий | № Част.» ("Surnames of the old ranks | General No. | Part No."), where the general one is the book number and the
part one is the folio number; such volumes have a table of contents "letter →
pages" at the beginning, and with it one can jump straight to the right place.

The master scan is at `rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_NNN/XXXX.jpg`
(3200×2133 or 3500×2764, about 400–800 KB). The previews in `/opisi2/` are 555×700,
with nothing to read.

## Three Stories That Came Out of This

**The founding of Novy Oskol.** File 210-6д-18 is the construction book of the town of Tsarev
Alekseyev, the very one that eight years later would be renamed Novy
Oskol. Yelena Chernova's list (rospis) extracted from it «Данило Левонтьев сын
Анпилов» (Danilo, son of Levonty, Anpilov); the alphabetical index independently places the surname on folio 34. Two different
scholarly reference aids, compiled at different times by different people,
converged on one file — and now we have an exact location.

**Khotmyzhsk.** In "Dom Predkov" Khotmyzhsk is represented by one single
line. The alphabetical index gives six folios for it in two files, and three of them —
23, 42 and 65 — fall within the construction book of the year 148, that is, the list of those
who came to build the new town on the Belgorod Line in 1640.

**Kursk and Oboyan in one file.** In book No. 10 the surname occurs twice: folio 267
falls in the estimate book of Kursk of the year 160, folio 425 in the enrolment
desyatnya for Oboyan of the year 162. This is exactly the pair of towns between which an
unresolved link hangs in the project: the Kursk record of 1636 and the Oboyan one of
1651, both assigned to Sergey Grigoryevich (P0052). The Oboyan part of the
file is only 28 folios — cheap to order.

## And the Main Dispute: Anpilov or Anpilogov

For the Novy Oskol statements of 1691–93 (file 210-6д-161) the "Dom
Predkov" index names three people: Anpilogov Grigory, Anpilogov Yakov and Anpilov
Khariton. The alphabetical index of the Razryad Archive gives **three folios in this file — 539, 540
and 546 — and all three under the surname «Анпиловъ» (Anpilov)**, without «-ог-» (-og-). Chernova's list for
the same Novy Oskol statements also reads all three as «Анпилов» (Anpilov) and
adds patronymics: Grigory and Khariton Kondratyevich, brothers, and Yakov
Danilovich.

In total, for Novy Oskol three independent readings — the 19th-century alphabetical index, Chernova's
list and the alphabetical index to the construction book of Tsarev Alekseyev — say «Анпилов» (Anpilov),
and one, the latest, says «Анпилогов» (Anpilogov). Meanwhile for Khotmyzhsk the same
index confidently writes «Анпилогов», and for Kursk «Онпилогов» (Onpilogov). It seems
the forms really are different and divided geographically, not only by the hand of
the scribe. Only an image of the folios themselves can settle this for good —
and now we know exactly which ones.

## Volume 127: Belgorod, 1685/86

Volume 127 is "volume V" of the alphabetical indexes, books **129–133**. The offset is simple: the volume folio
plus two equals the scan number. Each book has its own handwritten title
«Книги Бѣлогородскаго стола … за № N» ("Books of the Belgorod Desk … No. N"); the letter А begins right below it.

The letters А and О were viewed in all five books. Two hits.

**Book 131 — «Анпилоговъ» (Anpilogov), folios 39, 133, 184, 188.**
Scan [0137](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_127/0137.jpg). According to the inventory these are
the register lists (rospisnye spiski) of the town of Belgorod: of the year 194 on folios 1–299, countersigned by the dyak
Stefan Lunin, and of the year 196 on folios 300–426, countersigned by Klim Sudeykin. All
four of our folios lie in the first part — that is, in the **register list of Belgorod
itself for the year 194 (1685/86)**. A register list was compiled when a voivode was replaced:
the town, its fortifications, artillery (naryad), stores and the garrison by name.

This is the first trace of Anpilogovs in Belgorod itself. Until now Belgorod had been in
the project only as the name of a military district (razryad); according to the alphabetical indexes Anpilogovs lived in Khotmyzhsk,
Olshansk, Novy Oskol and Kursk.

**Book 132 — «Анпилоговы» (Anpilogovs), folio 20.**
Scan [0163](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_127/0163.jpg). The annual estimate
book of the towns of the Belgorod Desk for the year 194 (1685/86), first part, 902 folios;
the second part is file 133. The surname is written **in the plural**, so there is
more than one person on the folio. Folio 20 out of nine hundred is the very beginning of the book,
the first or second town section; which town it is, only the folio itself will show.

Files 131 and 132 are of one and the same year, 1685/86, and both belong to the Belgorod
Desk. If folio 20 of the estimate book turns out to be the Belgorod section, it will be the
same group of people as on the four folios of the register list.

**Book 130 — «Анпилоговъ» (Anpilogov), fols. 34, 167, and «Анпиловъ» (Anpilov), fols. 561, 567, 635, 1210.**
The annual estimate book of the towns of the Belgorod Desk for 7193 (1684/85), 1802 folios.
Both entries stand on the **left** pages of the spreads (scans
[0024](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_127/0024.jpg) and
[0026](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_127/0026.jpg)) — in the first pass I cropped
only the right half of the scan and did not see them. The inventory lists the countersignatures by folio:
folio 34 falls under the dyak Mikh. Zhedenov (fols. 1–95), folios 167, 561, 567 and 635 —
under the dyak P. Istomin (fols. 143–1082).

What matters is not the number of folios itself but the fact that **in one book the index separated two forms
of the surname into different lines**. The 19th-century scribe did not merge «Анпилогов» (Anpilogov) and «Анпилов» (Anpilov),
which means that in the book itself they are written differently. This is the first direct argument in
the dispute that until now rested on the Novy Oskol statements.

**Empty:** book 129 (census book of Voronezh and its district, 1684/85, 490 fols.) —
letter А; book 130 — letter О; books 131 and 132 — letter О; book 133 (second part
of the estimate book of 1685/86, 882 fols.) — А and О.

## A Spread Is Four Columns, Not Two

A mistake worth recording so that the next assistant does not repeat it. One
scan of an index is **a spread of two pages**, and each page has two
columns of surnames: four columns per scan. The folio number is in the top corner of the
**right** page; the left one is the verso of the previous folio. If the scan is cut down the
usual middle, half the surnames are left out of view. That is exactly how
both entries for book 130 in volume 127 were missed. After the correction, all the letters А and О in
volumes 127 and 132 have been gone through on both halves.

## Volume 132: Regimental Musters at the End of the Century

Volume 132 contains the indexes to books **170–181**, and it is arranged more conveniently than the others: the left
page of scan [0003](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_132/0003.jpg) has
a table of contents "letter → folio numbers of the volume". Books **176 and 177 share one index**
(the title is written exactly so: «за № 176 и 177», "for Nos. 176 and 177"), so there are eleven indexes for
twelve books. The "folio → scan" offset in this volume is **not constant**: at the start it is
folio + 2, by book 176 already + 4, from book 179 + 5 — one has to go by the folio
number in the corner of the right page.

Four hits.

**Book 171 is the only case where a folio can be localised down to a person.** The inventory
lists the countersignatures by folio, and both our folios, 250 and 252, lie within the section
232–262 countersigned by the voivode **A. Sinyavin**. So even before ordering, it is known
whose administration the entry belongs to.

**Book 173 is the most "telling" type of document.** An arrival list is a roll-call
at the muster point: who arrived and when, with what horse and weapons; at the end of the book there is
a separate list of netchiki, that is, those who did not turn up. Here it is the muster for the regiment of the boyar
**Boris Petrovich Sheremetev**. The same dyak P. Menshoy Isakov also countersigned the
arrival lists of 1691/92 against the Crimean khan (file 160, fols. 24, 34, 41).
Together with the collection book of 1701 this makes three documents of regimental musters within
ten years. A caveat is obligatory: files 160 and 173 have «Анпилогов» (Anpilogov), whereas file
171 has «Анпиловы» (Anpilovs), and under rule 2 of the project these forms are not identified with each other until
it is proved that the same people are meant. For now this is an observation about surnames in a single
military district, not an established line of service of one family.

**Empty:** the letter О in all eleven indexes of the volume without exception; the letter А in
books 170, 174, 175, 176+177, 178, 179 and 181.

## Volume 128: Carts and Horses, 1686/87

Volume 128 covers books **134–146**, and on the right page of scan
[0002](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_128/0002.jpg) it has a ready table of contents
«Литера — нумера листов» ("Letter — folio numbers"). Eleven columns of values for thirteen books:
books **140 and 141 share an index**, and book **144 has no index at all** — on
folio 290v it is written plainly: «Книги за № 144 алфавитъ не составленъ» ("No index has been compiled for book No. 144").

The "folio → scan" offset here is a trap. At the start of the volume it is folio plus three, because
scans 0003 and 0004 are two shots of the same folio 1: in the first, part of the right
page is covered by a cardboard label «Алфавиты Белогородскаго стола, 8» ("Indexes of the Belgorod Desk, 8"), in the second
it has been moved aside. After the inserted folio "232a" (scan 0236, blank) the offset becomes
plus four.

One hit.

**Book 135 — «Анпилоговы» (Anpilogovs), folios 4, 100, 105.**
Scan [0024](http://rgada.info/opisi/210-ukaz_128/0024.jpg). According to the inventory this is
*«Сборныя книги подводъ и лошадей „подъ хлѣбные и полковые всякіе припасы“ съ
служилыхъ и жилецкихъ людей разныхъ чиновъ и городовъ, вѣдавшихся Бѣлгородскимъ
столомъ»* (*"Collection books of carts and horses 'for grain and all regimental supplies' from
servicemen and townspeople of various ranks and towns under the Belgorod
Desk"*), 7195 (1686/87), 989 folios.

This type of document is rare for our sample. All the other files found — estimate
books, register lists, arrival lists, statements — are documents of **service**:
whoever serves gets into them. A collection book of carts apportions a duty in kind and therefore covers
**servicemen and townspeople (zhiletskie lyudi)** at once, that is,
it takes in both town and district residents. So far this is the only file where Anpilogovs
may turn out to be recorded as something other than servicemen.

The inventory lists the countersignatures by folio, and both sections can be localised: folio 4 lies under
the countersignature of **M. Kuzmin** (fols. 1–46), folios 100 and 105 under the countersignature of **Prince
A. Meshchersky** (fols. 92–154). Three folios in the first sixth of a nine-hundred-folio
book are most likely one or two town sections, not scattered mentions.

**Empty:** the letter О in all eleven indexes of the volume; the letter А in books 134, 136,
137, 138, 139, 140+141, 142, 143, 145 and 146.

One caveat that must not be omitted. On the title of book 134 the 19th-century scribe
noted: «Невѣдомости и цѣлости листовъ во всей книгѣ многихъ оригиналовъ
неразобрано, а въ алфавитѣ не внесено» ("Owing to the illegibility and incompleteness of folios throughout the book, many originals
have not been deciphered and have not been entered in the index"). So part of the book he simply could not
read and did not enter in the index. The absence of the surname from the index of this book proves
nothing.

### Book 151: Twenty-Seven Folios and the Word "and"

A distribution book of cash pay is a nominal roll: to whom, how much and for
what service money was paid out. Twenty-seven folios in one file is more than everything
volumes 121–128 and 132 together gave for any single file.

But something else is more important. This line is written as nowhere else:
**«Анфилоговы и Анпилоговы»** ("Anfilogovs and Anpilogovs") — the 19th-century scribe combined two forms of the surname in one
entry with the conjunction «и» ("and"). Until now we had seen the opposite: in book 130 of volume 127 and in
books 147 and 153 of this volume the forms stand on **different** lines, and in book 153 they even
fall under the countersignatures of different dyaks. Here, by contrast, the reference work itself says that they are
one and the same. Rule 2 of the project is not overridden by this — people can still be identified
only name by name — but the dispute has gained a documentary
argument from a side that had none before.

### Book 149: the Only Folio with a Known Town

The inventory lists file 149 by sections, and this is a rare stroke of luck: folios **25–105**
are taken up by a muster roll of servicemen of various ranks of the town of **KURSK** for the year 196
(1687/88), countersigned by the dyak Illarion Kolpakov. Our folio 57 lies within
it. So even before ordering, both the place and the type of document are known: a muster, where
name, rank, oklad, horse and weapons are recorded.

**Empty:** the letter О in all seven indexes of the volume; the letter А in books 150 and 152.

### Why This Matters More than the Number of Folios

"Rouble money instead of service" is a buy-out from personal service with money. A collection
book of this kind records exactly the turning point for which the whole
research was undertaken: a serviceman of a southern town stops going on campaign and
begins to **pay**. This is the very transformation of the servicemen of the Belgorod Line
into **odnodvortsy** ("single-homesteaders") — a tax-paying estate that half a century later was resettled to the south,
including to Timoshevka.

Until now all the files found through the indexes showed Anpilogovs **in service**:
estimate books, register and arrival lists, reviews, statements. Here for the first time
one can see how service comes to an end. And one sees it three times in a row — 1701, 1702–3,
1700–5 — that is, not as an isolated case but as a condition.

The inventory lists the countersignatures by folio, so most of the folios can be localised:
in book 182, folios 72–77 lie under the countersignature of the dyak **Grigory Sheblykin**
(fols. 1–232), and twelve folios from 296 to 567 under the countersignature of the dyak **Yevstafy
Muzhlanov** (fols. 233–573); in book 184, folios 39, 44, 78 and 164 under the countersignature of
the dyak **D. Belago**, folios 254 and 312 under the countersignature of **M. Zhedenov**, folio 328
under the countersignature of **Afanasy Ostafyev**; in book 185, folios 847 and 934 under the countersignature of
the voivode **R. Saburov**.

One detail of the handwriting worth remembering: **the order of numbers in these lines
is not strictly ascending**. In book 184 the last number, 164, is added after 328 —
clearly a later addition, made when the scribe found one more mention and appended it to the end of
the line. The same happens in book 182, in the four hundreds. So these lines were not put together in
one sitting, and one cannot rely on ascending numbers when reading them.

**Empty:** the letter О in all four books; the letter А in book 183.

## How This Is Represented in the Data

All the cards of this batch were made according to
[the rules for entering genealogical cards](/read/research/genealogy-data-entry-rules):

- every inventory card is `recordType: finding-aid-entry`, that is, a search
  hint rather than a Record about a person, and it does not go into the user-facing catalogue of
  records;
- every event has a `placeId` from the place index. Where the inventory says
  "towns under the Belgorod Desk" and no town is named, a
  regional tag `belgorod-razryad` is set with `geo.precision: region` and low
  confidence — the point is notional; the town will appear only from an image of the folio.
  A town is named directly only in file 131: Belgorod;
- the dyaks, voivodes and the commander named in the inventory are placed in separate
  `mentions[]` objects with roles, ranges of countersignatures and the historical reading in
  `nameAsTranscribed`. None of them becomes the subject of the card:
  `primaryPersonId` is empty, because the inventory does not name Anpilogovs at all;
- the forms «Анпилогов» (Anpilogov) and «Анпилов» (Anpilov) are nowhere normalised to «Ампилогов» (Ampilogov). This is rule 2:
  the surname from the document is the primary one until the identity of the person is proved.
  All the more so since an index entry is not even a documentary reading but a paraphrase by the hand of a
  19th-century scribe;
- for each scan and fragment a SHA-256 has been computed, `publicDisplay: false`.
